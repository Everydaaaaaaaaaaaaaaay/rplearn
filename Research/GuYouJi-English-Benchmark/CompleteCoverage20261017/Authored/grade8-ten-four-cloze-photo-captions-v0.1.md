# 初二十空四选：相册里迟写的说明

## 学生题面

阅读下面完整短文，从每题四个选项中为第1—10空各选最合适的一项。每题1分，共10分。

For Grandma's birthday, I planned to make a family photo book. I imagined clean pages, matching dates and short, clever captions. I would finish it in one weekend. Grandma lent me a box of photographs and said I could ask her about any of them.

At first, I hardly asked. I put the pictures in date order and wrote what seemed (1) ____. A woman standing beside a bus must be going on holiday. Three children with buckets must be playing at the beach. I liked how quickly the pages filled.

When Grandma looked at my first pages, she smiled, then went (2) ____. I thought she was tired. "We can look at the rest tomorrow," I suggested. She shook her head and pointed to the woman by the bus. "That was my first morning at work."

My neat caption was (3) ____. The bus had taken her to a factory, not a holiday town. The buckets in the next picture were for carrying apples. The children were helping their neighbours, far from any beach.

I wanted to explain that the photographs had given me no (4) ____. Then I remembered who had lent them to me. Grandma had already offered to help. I closed my notebook and began to (5) ____.

Grandma spoke slowly. Some names came easily; other details took time. Instead of filling every silence, I waited. When she was unsure of a year, we left a space rather than (6) ____ one. On the next visit, my aunt brought an old letter that helped us find the date.

The book took three weekends, not one. Its captions were no longer all the same length. Some were only a place and a date. Others held a short memory in Grandma's own words. The pages looked less (7) ____ than my original plan, but they felt much more like our family.

At her birthday lunch, Grandma showed the book to my cousins. She told one story, and they asked for another. I watched the photographs turn into a (8) ____ instead of a display.

Later, she thanked me for leaving room for her voice. I had expected praise for finishing (9) ____. What mattered to her was that I had taken time. I still enjoy making neat pages, but now I ask my questions (10) ____ advance, before I write.

### 全组回填

For Grandma's birthday, I planned to make a family photo book. I imagined clean pages, matching dates and short, clever captions. I would finish it in one weekend. Grandma lent me a box of photographs and said I could ask her about any of them.

At first, I hardly asked. I put the pictures in date order and wrote what seemed obvious. A woman standing beside a bus must be going on holiday. Three children with buckets must be playing at the beach. I liked how quickly the pages filled.

When Grandma looked at my first pages, she smiled, then went quiet. I thought she was tired. "We can look at the rest tomorrow," I suggested. She shook her head and pointed to the woman by the bus. "That was my first morning at work."

My neat caption was wrong. The bus had taken her to a factory, not a holiday town. The buckets in the next picture were for carrying apples. The children were helping their neighbours, far from any beach.

I wanted to explain that the photographs had given me no clues. Then I remembered who had lent them to me. Grandma had already offered to help. I closed my notebook and began to listen.

Grandma spoke slowly. Some names came easily; other details took time. Instead of filling every silence, I waited. When she was unsure of a year, we left a space rather than inventing one. On the next visit, my aunt brought an old letter that helped us find the date.

The book took three weekends, not one. Its captions were no longer all the same length. Some were only a place and a date. Others held a short memory in Grandma's own words. The pages looked less uniform than my original plan, but they felt much more like our family.

At her birthday lunch, Grandma showed the book to my cousins. She told one story, and they asked for another. I watched the photographs turn into a conversation instead of a display.

Later, she thanked me for leaving room for her voice. I had expected praise for finishing quickly. What mattered to her was that I had taken time. I still enjoy making neat pages, but now I ask my questions in advance, before I write.
