# 初一独立选做阅读三题：项目标签页调查

## 学生题面

独立选做阅读：阅读全部六段，回答3题；每题从A、B、C中选出最合适的一项。每题2分，共6分。文中学校调查和数据均为虚构，请只依据本文定义、材料及其明确限制作答。

When a class begins an online project, one useful page may lead to another. Soon a student can have many tabs open across the top of the screen. Some tabs are useful references, while others are forgotten. A school report looked at a pattern it called ‘lost-tab searching’. It did not treat every screen with many open tabs as a problem.

The report required two features together. A student opened a page for the current project and later forgot where to find it among the tabs. The student then spent at least five minutes opening the same search results again to recover it. Keeping ten tabs open did not count if the student could still find the needed page quickly.

Repeated searches take time that a class could use to read, compare ideas or discuss evidence. A student may finish with plenty of links but little time to examine them. Learning more about lost-tab searching could help teachers plan useful support for organising sources. The purpose is to improve access to information, rather than to reward students simply for opening fewer pages.

The fictional research team asked 2472 students about their work during the last three months. Students recalled how often they lost a project page and how long their repeat searches took. Teachers also described the project tasks. They did not record every click, so the study relied on students’ estimates for the time spent searching again.

Students who reported giving saved links clear names also reported less lost-tab searching. Those who relied only on remembering the order of tabs reported more. This was an association in reported behaviour, not proof that naming a link caused faster searching. Project length and students’ previous experience could also be related to the difference between the two groups.

The team suggested testing a short link-naming activity in a later project. Teachers could compare the time needed to recover a page before and after that activity. The present study did not perform that test or measure its effect. Its findings describe these reported school projects, not all online reading or a proven method for improving memory.
