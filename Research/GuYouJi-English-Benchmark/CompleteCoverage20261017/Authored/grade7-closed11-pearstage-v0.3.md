### B组（第4—7题）

#### The Pear Surprise

![虚构果园采果示意：成人、儿童与篮子，非照片](build/english-benchmark-authority-20261017/mixed754-media-v0.1/grade7-closed11-pearstage-orchard.svg)

Nora went to the orchard with her father to pick pears. She chose large yellow pears that tasted sweet. Her plan was to make a smooth pear sauce for the village food show. She wanted a neat label, a bright jar and no changes to her recipe.

‘Can I help?’ asked her younger brother Eli, holding a little basket beside the tree.

Nora pointed him towards a tree with smaller, sharper-tasting pears. ‘Pick over there,’ she said. She hoped he would stay busy and leave her own fruit alone.

Eli soon returned with a full basket. He held it up proudly, but Nora was already thinking about the perfect jar she would fill at home.

‘Put those by the door,’ she said, without looking closely. She thought the small pears would spoil the soft, sweet taste she wanted.

At home their father washed and cut the fruit. He put Nora’s pears into a pot on the stove and did all the work near the heat himself. Nora and Eli stayed at the table, preparing the jars.

![词汇图释：左pot，右stove，非烹调操作步骤](build/english-benchmark-authority-20261017/mixed754-media-v0.1/grade7-closed11-pearstage-pot_stove_gloss.svg)

Dad picked up the second basket, thinking it was part of Nora’s plan. Before she noticed, he had added some of Eli’s pears to the pot.

‘Those are the wrong pears!’ Nora cried angrily. Her face grew hot. ‘Now the sauce will taste terrible.’

Dad turned off the stove when the sauce was ready and let it cool. He invited Nora to try it, but she pushed her spoon away because she expected an unpleasant taste.

At last she took a tiny spoonful. She paused, then tried another. The sauce had a fresh flavour she liked much more than she had expected.

‘It is different,’ Dad said, ‘but different does not always mean worse.’ Nora looked at Eli’s worried face and began to smile.

She hugged her brother and wrote both names on the label. ‘I hope our surprise sauce wins a prize tomorrow,’ she said. The story ends before the food show, so nobody knows its result.

### C组（第8—11题）

A class is preparing a surprise puppet show for its visiting reading group. The pupils know the date, but they want to keep the final scene out of the public programme until the performance. These are ordinary project details, not personal secrets. The class has agreed that performers and the teacher may know the scene, while visitors will discover it during the show.

#### Why Can a Plan Become Tiring?

At first, keeping the ending off the noticeboard seems simple. Later, several pupils ask the same questions, and the organisers must remember which details are already public. They spend time checking the programme and correcting old copies. Even a friendly surprise can become tiring when there is no clear list of what has been announced.

#### What Happens When Helpers Work Together?

An organiser who tries to answer every question alone may miss rehearsal time. In this class, that happened to Sam: he worried about printing the wrong ending and checked the same page again and again. The report describes his experience during this project; it does not claim that every surprise plan affects every pupil in the same way.

Working with an agreed helper can **strengthen the bond with** that person. When Sam and another performer checked the programme together, they understood each other’s jobs better and felt closer as teammates. Their shared list also made it easier to keep the public copies consistent. The useful part was cooperation, rather than simply having information that other people did not have.

#### ____10____

What should a pupil do if a project detail becomes difficult to manage? In this class, the pupil can ask the team leader which helpers are on the agreed list. A new person should be added only after the group discusses it. Some details can be announced early, but the group should decide that together. This arrangement concerns their fictional show, not a rule about personal problems or safety.
