### B组（第4—7题）

#### The Peach Jar

![虚构果园采果示意：成人、儿童与两篮果实，非照片](build/english-benchmark-authority-20261017/mixed754-media-v0.1/grade7-closed11-peachwelcome-orchard.svg)

Lena had a clear plan for the summer fruit table at the community hall. She would make sauce from large golden peaches, the soft and sweet ones she liked best. At the orchard she passed over a smaller, firmer kind because it tasted sharper than her chosen fruit.

Her little brother Miles followed her between the trees. ‘Give me a job too,’ he said, lifting an empty basket.

Lena pointed to the smaller peaches. She asked him to collect those for another day and went back to selecting the biggest golden fruit for her sauce.

Miles filled his basket carefully. He was pleased that he could help, but Lena was busy picturing a perfect jar on the summer fruit table.

She set both baskets near the kitchen door. She forgot to tell Grandma that Miles’s fruit had a different purpose.

Grandma washed and cut the peaches. She placed a pot on the stove and took charge of the cooking herself. The children remained at the table with paper and pens, planning a label.

![词汇图释：左pot，右stove，非烹调操作步骤](build/english-benchmark-authority-20261017/mixed754-media-v0.1/grade7-closed11-peachwelcome-pot_stove_gloss.svg)

Grandma assumed the two baskets belonged to the same recipe. She added several smaller peaches to the pot before Lena saw what was happening.

‘That is not my plan!’ Lena cried angrily. She thought the sharper fruit had spoiled the taste she had worked so hard to create.

After cooking, Grandma let the sauce cool and offered her a spoon. Lena first said no, expecting a poor flavour. She was not refusing because she had already tasted it.

Then Miles tried a little and smiled. Lena agreed to take one small taste too. She found the new flavour fresh and enjoyable, quite different from what she had feared.

Grandma said, ‘Sometimes a surprise gives us a better idea.’ Lena looked at the two baskets and asked Miles to help her finish their label.

‘Maybe people will choose our jar for a prize at the fruit table,’ she said. The community event was still in the future. The story does not tell us what the visitors later chose.

### C组（第8—11题）

Birch Class is choosing a book as a welcome gift for a new reading partner. The partner knows there will be a gift, but the class wants the title to be a surprise. The teacher has agreed that the selection team and the class librarian may know it. The pupils are managing a project choice, not another person’s private experiences or concerns.

#### Why Can a Surprise Need Careful Work?

Several books have similar covers, so the selection team must check the title on every order form. They also need a public message that does not name the book too early. Keeping those versions separate takes attention. Rechecking the same detail can become tiring when the pupils have other class jobs to finish as well.

#### What Can an Agreed Partner Change?

At first, Theo prepared all the messages on his own. He worried about putting the title on the public notice and spent too long checking it. He missed part of his usual reading time. This fictional account concerns Theo’s project; it is not a study showing that all children react to surprise gifts in the same way.

Sharing a clear job can **strengthen the bond with** an agreed partner. When Theo and the librarian checked messages together, they listened to each other’s reasons and felt closer as classmates. Their cooperation helped them keep the forms consistent. They valued the way they worked together, rather than the fact that they knew a title before the receiver did.

#### ____10____

When a detail becomes hard to manage, this team asks the teacher which helpers may see it. If they need another helper, they discuss adding that person before sharing the title. The group can also agree to announce the title early. It is a decision for this gift project, not a general rule that pupils must keep personal difficulties or safety concerns hidden.
