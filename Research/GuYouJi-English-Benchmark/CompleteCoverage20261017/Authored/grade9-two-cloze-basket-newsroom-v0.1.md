### A篇（第1—5空）

Grandma brought her old shopping basket to our craft afternoon. One handle was broken, and I thought buying a new basket would be (1) ____ than repairing it. Grandma asked me to look inside. I saw a small flower painted there by my mother when she was a child. I finally understood why Grandma wanted to (2) ____ it. We tied on a new handle, keeping the painted part (3) ____ and fully visible. The work took longer than a trip to the shop, but the basket could carry things again. Its value did not come from its (4) ____. It came from the family (5) ____ it held.

### B篇（第6—15空）

When I joined the school newspaper, I thought good writing meant impressive words. I proudly handed in my first report. The editor asked: "What actually (6) ____?" My description sounded exciting but left the event unclear.

She gave me three notebook headings: what I saw, what someone told me, and what I needed to (7) ____. They separated evidence from guesses and showed how much I relied on one (8) ____.

Next, I visited students preparing a concert. One pupil said all the tickets had gone. I almost reported that as fact, then remembered to (9) ____ it. The teacher's list showed five tickets still available. The pupil had confused two events.

I corrected the number and (10) ____ where the information came from. The editor didn't lengthen my report; she made it (11) ____.

After publication, a reader thanked us: she could still get a ticket. That message was more (12) ____ than praise for difficult vocabulary.

I still enjoy choosing words, but ask whether they make things clearer. Uncertain facts are marked for (13) ____, rather than presented as settled. I would rather publish a shorter, accurate report (14) ____ a longer, misleading one. Good writing begins with paying (15) ____ to what is really there.

### 答案与逐项依据

1. B。A：与work took longer than trip相反；B：后文明确修理比去商店久，买新的更快；C：未说购买更困难，题述比较是耗时

2. B。A：她要修原篮不想换成另一个；B：母亲童年画花具有纪念意义，奶奶想保留旧篮子；C：拿来让大家看并修，不是藏起不见人

3. C。A：fully visible说明完全露出，与covered覆盖相反；B：画面要完整可见，不能缺失；C：只换提手，画面完整露出且不动

4. B。A：母亲童年彩绘就是历史来源，否认history与结论矛盾；B：对比新旧购买与亲情价值，价值不来自价格；C：家庭纪念正构成意义，不是否认meaning

5. C。A：画花不是家庭指令，未有说明书；B：没有家人争吵的情节；C：母亲童年图案唤起家庭回忆

6. B。A：What actually cost不能独立形成此处问事件经过的完整问句；B：编辑问实际发生什么，对照华丽描述没有交代事件；C：并未说有东西消失，非问失物

7. A。A：把已见、他人讲述、还需核查分开，属于证据核实；B：不是装饰未知信息来替代证据；C：未核信息要追查，不是遗忘后就能证实

8. C。A：一篇报道证据不依靠一种颜色；B：后文音乐会是下一篇报道，首篇不涉及单张票；C：single source指单一消息来源，承接someone told me

9. C。A：只是重复学生的话不能发现仍有五票，实际进行了核查；B：向老师看清单是核事实，不是为消息做广告；C：不直接报道尽售，而向教师核对清单，发现仍有五票

10. A。A：注明信息来源，可回查；B：作者按证据分层学习，藏来源与便于核查相反；C：来源是老师清单，不能凭空编来源

11. A。A：纠正数字并注明来源，没有加长而使报道更清楚；B：已经移除错误补清单事实，不是更不可靠；C：正文did not lengthen直接说明没有加长

12. B。A：购票成功感谢，没有伤害结果；B：读者真正用到准确消息，比生词夸奖更有价值；C：读者感谢说明价值，不是令作者困惑

13. A。A：尚未确定的事实标待核，而非直接说已确定；B：没有出售事实的活动，sale不表示待核查；C：未知真伪不构成庆祝事项，非与settled相对的核查步骤

14. A。A：would rather...than...固定比较结构；B：would rather...as...不构成此处偏好比较；C：unless引条件从句，此处后面是名词短语

15. C。A：pay agreement to不是此处注意的表达；B：pay permission to不是此处注意的表达；C：pay attention to为固定搭配，注意实际情况

### 全组回填

### A篇（第1—5空）

Grandma brought her old shopping basket to our craft afternoon. One handle was broken, and I thought buying a new basket would be quicker than repairing it. Grandma asked me to look inside. I saw a small flower painted there by my mother when she was a child. I finally understood why Grandma wanted to keep it. We tied on a new handle, keeping the painted part untouched and fully visible. The work took longer than a trip to the shop, but the basket could carry things again. Its value did not come from its price. It came from the family memories it held.

### B篇（第6—15空）

When I joined the school newspaper, I thought good writing meant impressive words. I proudly handed in my first report. The editor asked: "What actually happened?" My description sounded exciting but left the event unclear.

She gave me three notebook headings: what I saw, what someone told me, and what I needed to check. They separated evidence from guesses and showed how much I relied on one source.

Next, I visited students preparing a concert. One pupil said all the tickets had gone. I almost reported that as fact, then remembered to verify it. The teacher's list showed five tickets still available. The pupil had confused two events.

I corrected the number and recorded where the information came from. The editor didn't lengthen my report; she made it clearer.

After publication, a reader thanked us: she could still get a ticket. That message was more valuable than praise for difficult vocabulary.

I still enjoy choosing words, but ask whether they make things clearer. Uncertain facts are marked for review, rather than presented as settled. I would rather publish a shorter, accurate report than a longer, misleading one. Good writing begins with paying attention to what is really there.
