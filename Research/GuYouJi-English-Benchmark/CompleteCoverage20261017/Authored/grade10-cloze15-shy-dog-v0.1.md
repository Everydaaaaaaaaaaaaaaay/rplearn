### 答案与逐项依据

1. C。A：并非多个地址造成转头躲藏；B：没有说Pip另有主人，动物自有反应不等于归属变化；C：had other ideas表示实际反应与拍摄者设想不同；D：狗未另约会面，情节是躲镜头

2. B。A：此处是动作距离变化，不是更早离开时间；B：提高音量后更远离，志愿者随后要求轻声；C：away表示距离远离，不是向高处跳跃；D：若更靠近不符随后的降低声音安抚策略

3. C。A：未涉及进食时间安排；B：后文主动靠近玩具，问题是适应陌生人而非玩具；C：初见拍摄者，需时间适应陌生人；D：动物不理解成品照片，志愿者解释的是与初识人物相处

6. D。A：观察者仍坐在房内，不是无人空房；B：未描述拥挤能安抚他，陌生人多反而不受本文支持；C：没有光线变化的对照记录；D：轻声坐定后他趋于放松，在安静时回到玩具旁

### 全组回填

I offered to take photographs for our neighbourhood animal shelter. I imagined friendly dogs facing the camera. But Pip, the first dog, had other ideas. Whenever I lifted the camera, he turned away and hid beside a chair.

I tried calling his name more loudly, but he moved even further away. A volunteer who had cared for Pip for weeks asked me to lower my voice and sit. He needed time with strangers, she explained. I put the camera beside me and waited.

Ten minutes passed. I thought I was wasting the afternoon. Then Pip came out to examine a soft toy near my shoe. I reached for the camera, and he retreated. This time I understood: my sudden movement had interrupted a small moment of trust.

The volunteer suggested watching before trying again. I noticed that Pip returned to the toy whenever the room was quiet. So I sat still, with the camera ready in my lap. When he eventually settled down, I took one photograph without standing up.

The picture was not what I had planned. Pip was looking at the toy, not at me. Yet his ears were relaxed, and the volunteer said the photograph showed a side of him that visitors rarely saw. We decided to use it with a short description explaining that he was shy at first.

A week later, a woman came to meet Pip after seeing the picture. She did not expect him to rush towards her. She sat by the chair and waited for him to approach. The volunteer arranged further visits before any decision was made.

I had wanted a photograph that would make Pip look instantly friendly. What he needed was one that represented him honestly. The camera could record a moment; it could not create the trust I had tried to hurry. After that afternoon, I changed my approach. I asked volunteers about each animal and allowed more time for the process.

Some days I came home with only a few usable pictures. I no longer counted that as poor productivity. A successful photograph was not simply one that looked impressive. It was one made with enough respect for the subject's needs.
