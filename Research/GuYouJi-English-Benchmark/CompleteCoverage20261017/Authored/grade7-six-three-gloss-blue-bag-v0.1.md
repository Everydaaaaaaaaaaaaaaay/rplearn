### 全组回填

Mum's birthday was on Friday. On Tuesday, Dad and I decided to make her a blue bag. I hid the cloth when she came home because I wanted to keep the gift secret until her birthday.

Dad put a spool of thread on the table. I had never used one before. I pulled the thread hard, and several loops came off at once. Soon there was a knot.


"I'll get some new thread," I said. Dad stopped me. We had plenty; it only needed untangling. He showed me how to find the free end. I pulled gently while he held the spool. Working slowly took longer than pulling hard, but we did not make another knot.

We made a cloth strap and added a buckle so Mum could change its length. Dad showed me how the strap passed through it. I tried it twice, then explained the steps back to him.


When we tested the bag, one end of the strap came loose. I wanted to hide it inside the bag. "Mum might put her books in here," Dad said. "The strap has to hold them." We took out the weak stitches and made that part stronger.

On Thursday, the bag was ready. I still wanted to add flowers, stars and Mum's name. Dad pointed to the small space left on the front. Instead of crowding everything in, I chose just one flower. Its yellow centre showed clearly against the blue cloth.

Mum opened the gift on Friday. She put a book inside, adjusted the strap and carried it round the room. "You made this for me?" she asked. I nodded. The bag was not exactly like the one in my first drawing, but it worked.

Later, I found the knotted thread in our work basket. I kept a little piece beside my drawing. It reminded me that asking for help had allowed me to finish the bag, rather than give up after the first problem.
