### B组（第4—7题）

#### Two Baskets of Plums

![虚构果园采果示意：成人、儿童与两篮果实，非照片](build/english-benchmark-authority-20261017/mixed754-media-v0.1/grade7-closed11-plumgarden-orchard.svg)

Mara wanted to make plum sauce for the neighbourhood harvest display. At her uncle’s orchard she carefully filled a basket with large purple plums. They were sweet and soft, just the sort she had planned to use. She imagined visitors praising the smooth sauce in her jar.

Her cousin Finn arrived with a basket that seemed almost as big as he was. ‘I can collect some too,’ he offered.

Mara sent him towards a tree with smaller red plums. They tasted rather sharp. She thought he would enjoy collecting them without changing her own carefully chosen fruit.

Finn worked slowly and proudly brought his basket to the car. Mara put it beside hers but did not explain that the two kinds were meant to stay separate.

On the journey home she talked about the label she would design. Finn listened, hoping his work would be useful too.

In the kitchen, Uncle washed and cut the plums. He used the pot on the stove, while the children decorated labels at the far end of the table. Only Uncle handled the hot equipment.

![词汇图释：左pot，右stove，非烹调操作步骤](build/english-benchmark-authority-20261017/mixed754-media-v0.1/grade7-closed11-plumgarden-pot_stove_gloss.svg)

He thought both baskets were for the same sauce. He added some of Finn’s small red plums before Mara turned round and noticed.

‘You have mixed them!’ she shouted angrily. ‘The sharp ones will ruin my sauce.’ Finn looked down at his label.

When the sauce had cooled, Uncle offered Mara a taste. She first refused because she believed the mixture would be unpleasant. Nobody had asked her to try it while it was hot.

She finally touched a little to her tongue. To her surprise, she liked its bright flavour. She asked for another spoonful and showed Finn the finished jar.

‘A change can give us something we did not know we wanted,’ said Uncle. Mara quietly rubbed out her name on the label and made space for two names.

‘Perhaps our jar will get a ribbon at the display,’ she told Finn. They had not taken it there yet. Whether it received a ribbon is not told in this story.

### C组（第8—11题）

The pupils in Cedar Class are making a welcome map of their school garden for a new class. They plan to reveal a small story corner at the end of the walk. The route is a class surprise, not anyone’s private information. The teacher and the map team may see the whole route. Other pupils already know when the walk begins and where to meet.

#### Why Can Organising a Surprise Take Effort?

Keeping the corner off the public map requires attention. Whenever a path changes, the team must check both the working map and the visitor copy. Forgetting which version is public can lead to more checking. After several changes, the task may feel tiring even though everybody is looking forward to the garden walk.

#### How Can Teamwork Change the Experience?

At first, Ada tried to update the two maps alone. She spent her free reading time worrying that the wrong copy would go onto the classroom wall. She kept opening the same files to check them. This was one pupil’s experience of a made-up project, not evidence that surprise activities generally make children worry.

An agreed partner can **strengthen the bond with** a teammate through shared work. Ada and Max compared their maps and asked each other clear questions. They began to understand one another’s choices and felt closer as a team. The benefit came from patient cooperation; merely being the only two people who knew a detail did not explain it.

#### ____10____

If a pupil needs more help with this map, the team first checks who is allowed to see the route. They can invite another helper after agreeing together. They may also decide to publish a part of the route early. No pupil should secretly change the team’s agreed visitor copy. These are arrangements for their garden welcome, not advice about keeping personal or safety concerns to oneself.
