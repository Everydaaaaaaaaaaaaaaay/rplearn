# 初二六段三题四选：乐谱边上的疑问

## 学生题面

独立选做阅读：阅读全部六段和五处词语释义，回答3题；每题从A、B、C、D中选出最合适的一项，每题2分，共6分。故事事例为虚构，请依据本文作者的观点和具体语境作答。

We often imagine a curious person asking great questions about distant planets or ancient cities. Yet curiosity can begin much closer to home, with something we have seen many times. A familiar object may contain a detail we have never tried to explain. Noticing that detail can change an ordinary conversation into a small opportunity to learn.

For the writer, the first clue (线索) is the moment when something no longer fits what we thought we knew. We notice a gap (差距) in our understanding and want to find out what belongs there. We do not need to know the right question immediately. The important beginning is recognising that an explanation is missing, instead of passing over the detail without another thought.

In this fictional example, I was putting music sheets away with a friend from the school choir. She stopped beside one page and began to hum softly (轻声哼唱). ‘This mark asks us to return to that earlier part,’ she said, pointing with her pencil. I had seen the mark before but had treated it as part of the decoration. I asked her to show me how it worked. We followed the two places on this particular sheet and sang the short passage again. Suddenly, the printed mark had a purpose I could understand.

The discovery was small, but the change in my thinking was useful. Before our conversation, I had not realised there was anything to ask about that mark. My friend did not give me an entire course in music, and I did not become an expert in one afternoon. She helped me notice one missing connection. I could then look for an explanation and check it against the page in front of us.

Other people can help us notice such missing connections because they attend to different details. A classmate might ask why a picture has an unusual border; a visitor might wonder how a local place got its name. We can pause (暂停), listen and ask what made the person wonder. Agreeing immediately is less useful than understanding the question. Looking at the material together also helps us decide whether the suggested explanation actually fits, or whether another question remains.

This is one way to approach (处理) a new subject as a learner. We can notice uncertainty, ask for another point of view and check an explanation with the relevant material. No single conversation will answer every question. It may simply help us recognise the next one. In that sense, curiosity is a habit of continuing to look and ask, rather than a performance in which we must already know everything.
