### B组（第6—10题）

#### B1

During a forest survey, researchers noticed a very tall tree that remained alive after a lightning strike. Some smaller trees and climbing vines around it had died. The team recorded the damage and considered why a dangerous event might leave a survivor with fewer competitors.

The report offered a possible explanation for the survival. Electric current might pass away from the large tree through touching branches and vines. In that case, less energy would remain in the tree itself. This was a proposed route, not something the team had measured directly, and other explanations still needed investigation.

The recorded tree had a broad crown that rose above the surrounding plants. Its thick trunk stood in a newly opened gap. With fewer vines on its branches and fewer trees crowding beside it, the survivor had more space and could receive more light. The report did not claim that lightning benefits every tree.

Its precise age remained unknown. The wood examined by the researchers did not contain a continuous sequence of identifiable annual rings. Counting the marks they could see would therefore not provide a complete age. They could describe a long-lived tree without knowing the exact year it had started growing.

This account asks readers to consider how plants respond to a disturbance and how neighbours compete for resources. It also shows why an observation can support a new question before the explanation has been fully established.
