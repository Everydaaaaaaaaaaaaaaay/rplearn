## 作者核对

### 材料1完整原创脚本

M: I have packed the old tiles in one large box.

W: That may be too heavy to lift safely. Divide them between three smaller boxes and put padding between the layers.

M: I will. The extra boxes will also fit better in the car.

### 材料2完整原创脚本

W: The theatre has offered our club free tickets, but we would have to sit separately.

M: That would make discussing the production difficult.

W: Not necessarily. We could meet in the cafe afterwards. I would rather see the play for free than insist on sitting together.

M: You are right. Let us accept. We should tell members about the seating before they sign up.

W: I will include that in the message, along with the cafe address.

### 材料3完整原创脚本

M: I compared the two translations of the poem. The older one keeps the rhyme, but leaves out the image of the river.

W: And the new one?

M: It keeps that image, though it does not rhyme. I think the image matters more to the meaning.

W: Would you use only the new version in your presentation?

M: No. Showing both will make the choices clearer. I will also read the original line, so the audience can hear its sound.

W: Then explain the trade-off rather than calling one version completely wrong.

### 材料4完整原创脚本

W: The interviews for our local history project are nearly finished. Can we put them straight online?

M: Not yet. Two people agreed to classroom use only, and one wants her surname removed.

W: I thought signing the form covered everything.

M: The form had separate choices for classroom use and public sharing. We need to respect each person's choice.

W: Then I will mark which extracts can be shared. What about the spelling of place names?

M: Ask each speaker to check the transcript. The old map uses a spelling that some residents no longer recognise.

W: Should we correct their speech to match the map?

M: No. Keep what they said, and add a note about the two spellings. That disagreement is part of the evidence.

W: Good. I will prepare separate classroom and public versions.

### 材料5完整原创脚本

N: Our town's repair cafe has completed its first year. Volunteers looked at a hundred and eighty objects, but that figure includes items they could not fix. Ninety-two were repaired, while the owners of another forty learned what spare part they needed. The remaining forty-eight were beyond the team's equipment or skills. Please remember these distinctions when using the numbers in your reports. Next year we will introduce appointments for electrical items, because each check can take a long time. Clothes and simple mechanical objects can still be brought without an appointment. There is no charge, though donations help buy materials. The cafe cannot guarantee a repair, and owners should not leave objects at the door when volunteers are absent.
