During a school project, our group compared two ways of recording how often students used a bottle-refill point.

At first, we placed a sheet beside the tap and asked each user add a mark.

The number of marks were low, although the caretaker said that the area had been busy.

Rather than concluding that few students used the tap, we watched the process careful during one lunch break.

Several student filled their bottles but left without noticing the sheet, which was partly hidden behind a sign.

We moved the sheet and tested a second method that did not depended on users writing anything.

A volunteer counted each visit, while another observer checked that the same person was not counted two time during a single visit.

Comparing the records helped us recognised that the first method had missed visits, not that the tap itself had become more popular.

Before present our findings, we agreed that neither method were perfect and made a note of the remaining limits.

The teacher accepted our revision and asked us to to keep the limits clear in the final report.
