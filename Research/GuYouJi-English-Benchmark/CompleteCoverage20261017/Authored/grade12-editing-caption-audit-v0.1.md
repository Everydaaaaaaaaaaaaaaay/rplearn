### 一种完整修订

When our school prepared an exhibition of old photographs, I joined a team that checked the captions against the archive. We soon found that three photographs had been placed under the wrong dates. A handwritten note suggested that the oldest picture was taken outside the first school building. Instead of accepting the note immediately, we compared the window shapes with a second picture. The volunteer who showed us the second picture had worked in the archive for years, and she explained her doubts patiently. She asked us to look for evidence in the building records rather than guess from the clothes people wore. One record included a useful sketch, but its date was difficult to read. After examining the original page, we agreed that the caption should mention the uncertainty. We did not present the suggested date as a fact, which made our account more careful rather than less useful. Visitors could then see both our conclusion and the evidence on which it was based, and several said that this approach was clearer.
