## 作者核对

### 材料1完整原创脚本

W: The new footpath looks shorter on the map. Shall we take it to the station?

M: It is shorter, but the last section has no lighting. By the time we leave the concert it will be dark.

W: Then we should use the main road tonight, even if it takes ten minutes longer.

M: Agreed. We can try the footpath on our way to the market on Saturday morning.

### 材料2完整原创脚本

M: The audience survey says the afternoon performance was too quiet. Should we turn up the speakers for the evening?

W: Look at where those people were sitting. Most were behind the pillar, while people near the front said the sound was clear.

M: So a louder setting might make the front rows uncomfortable.

W: Exactly. Let us test the speaker position first. Move one slightly sideways, and ask someone to listen from each section.

M: We have half an hour before the doors open.

W: Then test one change, make notes and leave enough time to restore the old position if it is worse. We should not keep experimenting after the audience arrives.

### 材料3完整原创脚本

W: The map for our cycling group marks a café beside the canal. Can we stop there for lunch?

M: It seats only eight people indoors, and there will be fourteen of us. I called to ask whether they could serve us in two groups.

W: What did they say?

M: They can prepare packed lunches if we order by Thursday. We could collect them and eat at the covered picnic area a little farther along.

W: That sounds better than leaving half the group outside waiting. What about people who want to bring their own food?

M: That is fine. I will ask everyone to choose on the booking form, so we order only what is needed.

W: And tell them the picnic area has no drinking-water tap. They should fill their bottles before we set off.

### 材料4完整原创脚本

M: The actor playing the uncle is ill. I suppose we must cancel Friday's performance.

W: Not necessarily. Lena knows the part from rehearsals, but she has never performed it in front of an audience.

M: Could she read from a script? It might look unfinished.

W: We can explain the change honestly at the start. I would rather let her hold the script than make her spend two days memorising every line under pressure.

M: The other actors will need to adjust their movements too.

W: Yes. We will rehearse the scenes with her this afternoon, especially the doorway scene where timing matters.

M: Should we shorten that scene?

W: Only if the movement cannot be made safe and clear. Do not cut the character's explanation merely to hide the replacement.

M: I will contact the group and ask them to arrive early.

W: Please also tell the ticket desk. People should hear about the change before they decide whether to attend.

### 材料5完整原创脚本

N: The recordings in this room come from a project on local accents. Volunteers read a short standard passage and then described a childhood memory in their own words. We used both tasks because reading the same words allows comparison, while an unscripted memory may reveal features that disappear in careful reading.

These recordings do not represent every voice in the district. Most volunteers heard about the project through one community centre, so people who rarely visit that centre may be missing. The map shows where speakers said they grew up, not where a particular accent begins or ends. Neighbours can speak differently, and people may change their speech in different situations.

Please use the headphones rather than playing the clips through your phone. The speakers agreed to use in this exhibition; that agreement does not automatically permit visitors to upload copies elsewhere. If you would like to study a recording further, speak to the project team about the appropriate access arrangements. We can discuss requests without assuming that public listening is the same as unrestricted reuse.
