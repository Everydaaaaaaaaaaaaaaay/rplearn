### B组（第6—10题）

#### B1

A forest report began with an unusual survivor: a large tree stood green after lightning had struck it, while several smaller plants nearby were badly damaged. The field workers found similar cases and asked how surviving a strike might change the competition around a tree.

One suggested explanation involved the route taken by the electric current. It might travel along touching branches and climbing vines and then reach neighbouring plants. If less energy stayed in the large tree, the damage to it might be smaller. The team had not directly measured this route, so the report treated it as an explanation to investigate.

The tree chosen as an example had a wide crown high above the surrounding growth. Its trunk was much thicker than the saplings around it. After the strike, dead vines no longer covered many of its branches, and some nearby competitors were gone. More light could now reach its remaining leaves.

The researchers did not know its exact age. The piece of wood they examined lacked an unbroken set of clearly annual rings. They could not turn a count of those visible lines into a reliable birthday. The report described the tree as old without claiming a precise year in which it began growing.

The study account focuses on plants living together and the effects of a sudden disturbance. Its observations raise questions about survival and competition, rather than establishing that every tree will respond in the same way.
