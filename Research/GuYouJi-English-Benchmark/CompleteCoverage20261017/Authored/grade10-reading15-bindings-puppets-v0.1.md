# 高一完整阅读四篇十五题：书单、偶戏展、运画车与提示研究

## 学生题面

阅读A—D四篇的全部材料，回答第1至第15题；每题从A、B、C、D四个选项中选出最合适的一项。每题2.5分，共37.5分。书籍、人物、活动、产品及研究均为虚构。

### A组（第1—3题）

Four members of the North Quay Book Club introduce imaginary books they have recently enjoyed. Their choices range from a graphic novel to a journey in diary form. Send the club a recommendation of your own for next month’s readers’ page; describe what makes your choice distinctive.

#### A1

A Door in the Rain — a graphic novel by Etta Noll
A delivery rider keeps finding the same painted door in different streets. The illustrations give clues that the characters’ speech sometimes misses. Reader Amir recommends looking closely at the edges of each frame before turning the page. Although the setting seems mysterious, the final explanation grows from the neighbours’ shared effort to protect a local artwork, rather than from magic.

#### A2

One River, Many Names — a travel diary by Sami Orr
Orr follows a small river from its source to the sea, recording a new entry each evening. Meetings with ferrymen, walkers and people living beside the water change the questions Orr asks. Reader June values the admission that a traveller sees only part of a place. Maps indicate the journey, but this is a personal account, not a guide to safe river travel.

#### A3

Under the Stage — a memoir by Faye Dem
Before becoming a set builder, Dem worked out of sight in a travelling theatre. She remembers hurried changes, wrong measurements and a patient supervisor who asked her to explain every design choice. Reader Noor says the account made backstage work feel important without making it look easy. Readers wanting a famous actor’s life story should choose something else.

#### A4

The Longest Afternoon — a verse novel by Cal Rinn
Short poems tell a continuous story about two cousins waiting for a relative’s delayed arrival. A familiar room gradually becomes strange as they invent competing explanations for the delay. Reader Leo likes how the cousins revise those explanations when messages arrive. Unlike a collection of unrelated poems, the book follows one afternoon from the first missed bus to the final knock at the door.

### B组（第4—7题）

#### B1

At the fictional Alder House Arts Centre, a new exhibition pairs traditional shadow puppets with moving sculptures made by design students. Visitors can look behind each screen to see the rods and joints. The organisers want the exhibition to reveal a working relationship between older performers and young makers, rather than present tradition as a collection of silent shapes.

The partnership began when puppeteer Mara watched a student’s model bird. Its painted wings resembled those on a puppet from her company, but they moved independently, producing a jerky flight. Mara demonstrated how a single rod could make two wings rise together. The student retained the pattern but rebuilt the movement around that connection. Alongside the finished sculpture stands the awkward first model, so that visitors can compare what changed.

Other teams went in different directions. One turned a sliding stage doorway into a panel that reveals several scenes as it moves. Another used the shape of a puppet’s hand to make a handle easier to grip. None of the teams claims that these objects can replace a live shadow performance. They are experiments in carrying a practical idea into a new setting.

The exhibition’s publicity is deliberately **restrained**. Instead of promising a revolution in design, the leaflet invites visitors to notice one small change and discuss it. Each label names both partners and records an idea that each contributed. “If only the finished object gets attention, people may think the student worked alone,” says curator Ivo. “We want the conversation to remain visible.”

During the opening weekend, visitors left sketches of movements they wanted to understand. The centre will use these requests to choose demonstrations for a later workshop. It has not yet measured whether visitors remember the ideas, but the requests give the organisers something more specific than a simple count of people through the door.

### C组（第8—11题）

#### C1

An oversized drawing folder can be surprisingly difficult to carry across a campus. The fictional PaperPort cart offers art students a way to move large, flat work while leaving their hands free to open a door. The description here belongs to an invented product campaign; it is not a report of independent trials or a claim about a real manufacturer.

PaperPort has a wide vertical frame fitted with two soft straps. Slip a closed portfolio into the frame, tighten the straps, and pull the cart by its height-adjustable handle. The wheels sit outside the frame so that a broad folder does not rub against them. The lower support folds upward when the cart is empty, allowing it to hang on a wall hook. A removable cover is included for light rain, though the maker specifically excludes heavy rain and immersion from its protection claim.

The handle has three marked height settings. Pressing a large side button releases it; there is no small screw to loosen. In the campaign’s example, a student wearing paint-stained gloves changes the setting without removing them. The example highlights an accessible control, not a medical claim that the cart is suitable for every person with limited hand movement. Users should check whether they can operate the button comfortably.

The basic pack costs  sixty fictional credits and carries a stated maximum load of eight kilograms. It includes the cover and a sheet showing how to secure different portfolio sizes. It does not include a portfolio. Replacement straps can be ordered separately, and the wheels can be removed with the supplied tool if a worn wheel needs changing. There is no motor or battery to charge.

Would PaperPort fit your routine? Measure your folder before ordering: the supported width range is printed beside the purchase button. The cart is intended for reasonably smooth paths and cannot make steps disappear. If you often carry drawings between ground-floor studios, however, its folding support and simple controls may remove one awkward part of the journey. The campaign invites prospective buyers to try an unloaded model at the campus shop before choosing.

### D组（第12—15题）

#### D1

A ringing reminder can attract attention, but can a silent visual change do the same job without interrupting a workshop? A fictional team at Redmere Institute tested a thin border that slowly changed colour around an on-screen drawing task. The study considered whether adults noticed a break reminder, not whether taking breaks improves health or the quality of their artwork.

Forty-eight volunteers attended the same introductory digital drawing session. By random assignment, half saw a small written break reminder appear after twelve minutes. The other half saw the same words in the same place, surrounded by a border that gradually changed from grey to blue. Both messages remained on screen for thirty seconds. Participants had been told that breaks were optional and that using one would not affect their payment.

The colour-border group was more likely to notice the message, according to a question asked immediately after the session. The groups did not differ clearly in how many people actually paused their work. Thus the result concerns awareness of this reminder in these conditions. It does not justify advertising the border as a way to make everyone take a break, still less as a treatment for a health problem.

Interviews suggested one reason for the gap between noticing and acting. Some volunteers said they had seen the message but wanted to finish a line before stopping; by then they had forgotten it. Others simply preferred to continue. These accounts show why the team recorded the two outcomes separately. A system can succeed in getting a message seen without achieving the action its designer hopes will follow.

Reviewer Professor Olin found the separation of outcomes useful. Nevertheless, she pointed out that one short session with adults cannot reveal what repeated reminders would do over a month. People might become used to the border, or it might remain helpful. She recommended longer tests in different tasks before the design was adopted widely. The team is preparing such a trial and has not offered a commercial product.
