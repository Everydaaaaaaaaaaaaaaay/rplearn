### 全组回填

Our costume club was making jackets for a school play. I had drawn a neat design with hidden pockets and wanted every jacket to look identical. When a performer named Lena asked for an extra opening in her pocket, I refused. Any visible difference, I thought, would spoil the design.
Lena did not argue. She asked me to watch a rehearsal before I made a final judgement. In one scene, she had to pass a letter to another character without turning towards him. Wearing my sample jacket, she repeatedly fumbled for the opening. Her hand caught in the lining, and the pause broke the flow of the scene.
Only then did she show me why she had asked for a change. An earlier wrist injury limited how far she could turn her hand. She could perform the movement comfortably with a wider side opening. The problem was not a lack of effort; she had practised far longer than I realised. My jacket was demanding a movement that she could not comfortably make.
I felt ashamed about refusing so quickly. But Lena was more interested in finding a solution than receiving an apology. We pinned back part of the lining and tried again. The letter passed smoothly. From the audience seats, the extra seam was barely noticeable.
At our next club meeting, I brought both versions instead of hiding the altered one. I explained the reason behind the change and asked the other performers to test their jackets. Two requested changes of their own. Rather than treating those requests as interruptions, we wrote them beside the measurements.
The finished jackets still belonged to one production, but they were no longer exact copies. On opening night, I watched the letter change hands without a pause. That small movement felt more satisfying than the perfectly straight lines in my first drawing. I had confused consistency with uniformity. A good shared design could make room for difference. Now, before I reach for a needle, I listen. The person wearing a costume has knowledge that a drawing cannot provide. Leaving space for that knowledge has made my work less rigid and far more responsive.
