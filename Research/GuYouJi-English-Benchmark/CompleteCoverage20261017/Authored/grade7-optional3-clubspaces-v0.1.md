# 初一独立选做阅读三题：社团名额调查

## 学生题面

独立选做阅读：阅读全部六段，回答3题；每题从A、B、C中选出最合适的一项。每题2分，共6分。文中学校调查和数据均为虚构，请只依据本文定义、材料及其明确限制作答。

A school club may look full on its booking page while several chairs remain empty at the meeting. Students waiting outside cannot always take those places, because the organiser needs time to prepare materials. A group of schools wanted to understand why this happened. Their report gave a name to one booking pattern: ‘unused holding’. Not every absence belonged to that pattern.

Two features had to appear together. A student kept bookings for two clubs that met at the same time. That student then missed one meeting without cancelling the booking beforehand. Booking two clubs on different days did not count. Neither did missing a single meeting because a bus was late, if the student had no second booking for that time.

Empty places can waste paper, tools and volunteers’ preparation time. They can also stop an interested student from joining an activity. Studying unused holding could help organisers understand when places remain unavailable and plan a better release system. It would not tell them that every absent student was careless: there can be many other reasons for missing a meeting.

In this fictional survey, 1926 students answered questions about the past three months. They listed bookings, meeting times and any cancellations. Organisers supplied attendance lists for the same period. The team compared the lists with the students’ answers, but did not observe why each student made a choice. Students were asked to report their choices, not explain their friends’ behaviour.

Students who said they booked quickly before reading meeting times reported more unused holding. Students who checked their weekly timetable reported less. The survey found an association, not evidence that a timetable check by itself prevents missed meetings. The two groups may differ in other ways, such as how often their transport arrangements change after school.

The team proposed a reminder showing both club names when meeting times overlap. A future trial could examine whether the reminder helps places become available earlier. No such trial was included in this survey. Its results concern the participating schools, so organisers elsewhere would need to check their own booking patterns before changing local arrangements.
