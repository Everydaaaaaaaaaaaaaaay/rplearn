# 初一四篇十五题：坡地房、猫日记与影子门

## 学生题面

阅读A、B、C、D四组完整材料，回答第1至第15题；每题从A、B、C、D四个选项中选出最合适的一项。每题2分，共30分。家庭、人物、猫日记、社团活动和幻想故事均为虚构。B组最后一条12 a.m.明确为次日开始的午夜。

### A组（第1—3题）

A home can make use of its surroundings in different ways. These children tell us about three homes and their everyday routines.

#### A1

Ada
Our home is built into a hillside. The hill behind it shelters the rooms from strong wind, which is the reason for the design. The front opens onto a small yard. Our windows face the valley, so I can watch the morning light reach the trees below. The roof is covered with grass, but we never play ball up there.

#### A2

Luis
Our family lives above a bakery. Before school, I sometimes help put paper bags on the counter, but only my parents operate the big oven. The shop is closed on Mondays, so there is no fresh bread for sale that day. My bedroom is at the back of the building, away from the street and the early customers.

#### A3

Mei
We live in a flat with a **terrace**, an open, flat area outside our rooms where we can sit. There is a low wall around it and space for two chairs. We grow tomatoes in pots there, not in a large garden. On fine evenings my family eats outside. When it rains, we take the chairs indoors and eat in the kitchen.

### B组（第4—7题）

#### B1

Today my human is staying home to finish a model ship. I, Dot the cat, am sure she will need a skilled assistant. This is my diary.

5 a.m. — I found a little paper flag on the table. It moved when the window opened. At last, a new game for me! I climbed onto a chair to watch it closely. My human was still asleep, so nobody told me to leave the table alone.

7 a.m. — My human came in and put the flag inside a drawer. She said it belonged on her ship, not between my paws. I could not see why the ship should get the best toy. I followed her to breakfast, still hoping she would change her mind.

8 a.m. — After breakfast, my human carefully painted the ship. I sat beside the table and waved my tail. She moved my chair back, saying she needed room for her brushes. A true assistant should be close enough to see the work, I thought.

3 p.m. — I have already carried my cloth mouse into the empty box beside the table. I played there for ten minutes. I am going to nap by the window later, but I am still awake now. The box makes a fine small boat for a cat.

9 p.m. — The model ship was finished. My human admired it and showed it to a friend on a screen. She thanked me for being quiet in my box. Quiet? I had been very busy! She gave me a pat, but the paper flag was still on the ship, beyond my reach.

12 a.m. (start of the next day) — This midnight starts the next day. My human has put the model on a high shelf and turned out the light. She thinks we made a ship together. I wanted to play with that moving flag. We clearly had different plans for the day.

### C组（第8—11题）

#### C1

Our fictional club has invented a table game called Paper Disc. We slide folded paper discs towards circles drawn on a smooth board. The centre circle gives the most points, but a disc that slides off the far edge scores nothing. Players can practise different gentle pushes before a round. There is no official world championship for this invented game.

In our club version, a player must keep the disc behind the blue line until the turn begins. Moving it nearer the target first is not allowed. Once a disc has stopped, the leader writes down its score before the next player takes a turn. The same board and starting line are used throughout a round, so players can compare their attempts.

A fast push is not always a useful push. The player has to notice the surface and choose how far to slide the disc. Like chess, the game rewards thinking about a move before making it. Unlike chess, it also asks you to control a small movement of your hand. Watching another player can give you an idea, but copying every push may not work for you.

What attracts me is the chance to adjust a plan after seeing a result. If you enjoy trying a careful idea and then changing it, Paper Disc is worth a simple trial. The aim is not to use the greatest possible force. There is room for a thoughtful beginner as well as an experienced player.

### D组（第12—15题）

#### D1

Eli reached a stone door on the way to the lantern garden. The lock had no keyhole. A sign said, “Only the owner’s shadow can open this door.” Eli’s shadow had been changing all morning: first it looked like a tall tree, then like a tiny umbrella. He needed it to look like him again. Finding lunch could wait; food would not open this door.

He looked among the stones beside the wall. In a round hollow he discovered a yellow lantern with a handle shaped like a leaf. A small purple beetle stood on it, polishing a pair of glasses. Eli waited until the beetle looked up. “What shape is your shadow?” it asked.

“▲,” said Eli. “Each time I look, it seems to have become something else. I cannot describe the one behind me without turning round again.”

“Then you have not been paying attention,” replied the beetle sharply.

Eli described the tree shape and the umbrella shape. He asked whether the lantern could help him find his own shadow.

“Tell me what it is now,” said the beetle. It repeated the same question, though Eli had just explained why that was difficult. It put on its glasses and began polishing the lantern again.

Eli decided that this conversation was going nowhere and took a step away. “Come back,” called the beetle. “I have not told you what the lantern does at night.” Eli returned, interested despite his annoyance. A tiny light appeared inside the lantern, but the stone door remained shut. He had not entered the garden, and he still did not know how to make his shadow stay in one shape.
