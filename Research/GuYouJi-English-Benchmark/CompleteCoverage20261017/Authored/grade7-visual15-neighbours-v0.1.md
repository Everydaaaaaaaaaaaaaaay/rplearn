### A组（第1—3题）

#### A1

Cedar Hall holds its Neighbours’ Day on the fourth Sunday in April 2029. The hall in Oak Lane uses April 15 for its own event. Both are fictional local celebrations. Families at Cedar will share short stories about someone who helped them during the year, and the children may draw a picture to go with a story.

The organisers borrow chairs, put out cups and check that new visitors can find the hall. They do much of this work before the stories begin. A quiet helper may be easy to miss when everybody is listening to a speaker. This year a large notice will name each organiser and describe one useful thing that person did. No family needs to bring a costly present.

### B组（第4—6题）

#### B1

My school is beside a wood outside the town. It has four classes, and its grass play area has no fence. Mr West, our maths teacher, uses games about shapes to make us think. We like working out where to stand to make a circle. He is friendly enough for us to call him Uncle West.

Ms Dale is our English teacher. Her songs and short stories make lessons enjoyable. **They** also help us remember new English words, because we hear the words in a familiar setting. We sometimes ask her to tell the same story again. Our PE teacher, Mr Lake, likes the trees near the play area.

Here is a picture of all four of us. Mr Lake sits on a low branch. Mr West is the man wearing dark sunglasses. Ms Dale and I stand below the tree. I have a small cap and am shorter than each of the teachers. No one has an umbrella in the picture.

### C组（第7—10题）

#### C1

A language club in our story teaches a sound routine. When a word is new, the member hears it more than once and remembers its sound before trying to say it. The teacher explains that the printed shape is not the only thing to learn. The club wants members to connect a sound with a meaning they can use.

Its games return to familiar words on different days. Children listen, find the right picture and use the word again. This repeated practice is part of the club’s routine, rather than a promise that every word will be remembered after one try. The account describes this group, not a test proving how all children develop memory.

Nico and May heard two home languages when they were little, before they entered primary school. Some other members hear their new language mainly in the club. Their many other school lessons leave less time for it during the week. The club uses short opportunities to practise again, instead of asking them to remember a long list after a single look.

### D组（第11—15题）

Read the three poems below.

#### D1

Your small hand leaves the wooden train;
The evening room is warm and still.
I close the curtain against the rain;
A moonbeam rests upon the sill.

Your little shoes can rest below;
The blanket keeps your shoulders warm.
I stay beside you as you slow,
And night lies quiet round your arm.

#### D2

Dear Mother, when you feed me near
The garden wall, I hear your song.
You stroke my fur and keep me close.

I kiss your nose before I rest;
It is my last small kiss each night.
Your gentle care is what I know.

#### D3

I count the cups before our meal,
And sweep the crumbs beneath the chair.
I take clean plates with careful hands.

I fold the towels and put them right,
Then bring a cushion for us to share.
I like to help at home tonight.
