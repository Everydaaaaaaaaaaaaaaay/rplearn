# 高二六选四：工作场所里的小词典

## 学生题面

阅读短文，从A—F六个句子中为四个空白各选出最合适的一句。每个句子只能使用一次，其中有两项多余。

During her first month at a printing company, an intern called Mira began keeping a list of expressions she did not understand. She expected most of them to be technical terms with clear definitions. Instead, she found that a phrase could change its meaning between the office upstairs and the workshop below. 'A red job', for instance, might concern a deadline, an account or the colour of the paper.

(1) ____ For the sales team, a red job was an order whose payment had not arrived. For the printers, it was an order that had to be interrupted so that another one could be finished first. Neither group thought its use was unusual. Each assumed that the other people in the company knew exactly what was meant.

Mira initially tried to choose one correct definition for each expression. That approach produced arguments without making the list much more useful. She then changed her method. (2) ____ The examples showed how a term entered a conversation and what the listener was expected to do next. A definition that sounded complete on its own sometimes became misleading as soon as it was placed beside an actual exchange.

(3) ____ She added the relevant team, a typical situation and a short example to every item. New staff could then recognise a difference in usage without having to accuse a colleague of speaking incorrectly. The list became a guide to asking for clarification, rather than a test of whether somebody had memorised the approved vocabulary.

By the end of the placement, Mira had stopped calling the list a company dictionary. The title suggested an authority that she did not claim. (4) ____ Experienced workers began contributing situations in which they too had misunderstood an instruction. Their examples made the guide longer, but also made it easier for a newcomer to admit that something was unclear.
