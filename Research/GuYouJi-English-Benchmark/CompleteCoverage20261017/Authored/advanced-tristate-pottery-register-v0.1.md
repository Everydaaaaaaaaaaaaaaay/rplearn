### An archaeological register reconsidered

In a fictional account of a small archaeological survey, an old pottery register became the starting point for a new investigation. Each entry gave a crate number, a brief description and the depth at which a fragment was reportedly found. The register’s first owner had studied several different collections, but the account began with the decision to re-examine this particular set of entries. It did not trace the owner’s childhood or early education. The research question concerned whether the recorded depth categories were consistent enough to support a comparison between excavation areas.

The new team found that two excavators had used different meanings for one depth label. To establish this, they compared dated field sketches with register entries that referred to the same crate numbers. The sketches preserved measurements that had been reduced to a single descriptive term in the register. Matching the numbers made it possible to recover the distinction without assuming that identical wording necessarily indicated identical practice. Entries without a corresponding sketch were kept in the catalogue, but the team marked their interpretation as provisional.

When publishing the revised catalogue, the team retained the old descriptions beside the proposed interpretations. Readers could therefore see which changes depended on additional evidence and which entries remained unresolved. The authors rejected a suggestion that they should replace the original wording throughout with a uniform modern label, since doing so would conceal the uncertainty they had uncovered. They also warned against treating a tidier catalogue as evidence that every fragment’s original position had become known. The revised document was intended to expose the limits of the record as much as to improve its usefulness.
