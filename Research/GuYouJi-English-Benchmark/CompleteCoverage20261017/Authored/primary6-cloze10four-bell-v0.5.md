## 作者核对

### 全组回填

My uncle gave me his old bicycle last month. The wheels were strong, but the bell made almost no sound. I wanted to use the bike for a ride on Saturday, so I took it to Aunt Rosa's repair shop on Friday, the day before.

Aunt Rosa turned the bell in her hand. 'There is a loose screw,' she said. She put it in a small round dish which she usually used for soup, so it would not roll off the work table. I watched carefully while she cleaned the parts. Then she asked me to pass her a screwdriver. She used it to tighten the screw.

'Now try it,' she said. I pressed the bell, and we heard a clear ring. I smiled. The bell did not need a new part; it only needed a little care.

Before leaving, I asked how much I should pay. Aunt Rosa said, 'Help me sort these tools by size.' I made one row across the table. I put the longest ones on the left and the shortest ones on the right. It took ten minutes.

On Saturday I rode to the park. The bell worked well, but I still slowed down near people. A loud bell is useful; careful riding is more important than a loud bell.
