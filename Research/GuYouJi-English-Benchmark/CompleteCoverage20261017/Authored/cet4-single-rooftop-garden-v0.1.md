# 四级：屋顶菜园的分工

### Passage 1

When the fictional Orchard Court opened, every flat had a narrow balcony. Residents could decorate their own space, but the building’s manager did not allow objects to hang over the railings. Mara wanted to grow vegetables and soon discovered that her balcony received little direct sunlight. Her neighbour’s balcony was brighter, but he travelled frequently and could not water plants regularly.

They began with a simple exchange. Mara placed two pots on his balcony and watered his plants while he was away. The pots were still hers, and neither person gained a general right to enter the other’s home. They arranged visits in advance and kept the shared activity separate from their private living rooms.

Other neighbours became interested. The manager suggested using a sunny corner of the roof for a small shared garden. A gardening company offered matching containers, but residents chose different sizes that could be moved without special equipment. They also kept a clear path to the roof door. Growing the largest possible quantity of vegetables was not their only concern.

The first watering schedule assigned one household to each day. It looked fair on paper, yet some residents worked night shifts and found morning visits difficult. Others could water plants but could not carry full cans upstairs. The group replaced identical daily duties with smaller tasks: checking soil, filling containers and bringing water. People selected duties they could manage rather than receiving the same assignment.

At the end of summer, the harvest was modest. Mara nevertheless valued the conversations that had grown around the garden. She also noticed that one neighbour was quietly doing most of the planning. The group decided to rotate that responsibility as well. Sharing a space had created useful opportunities, but cooperation required more attention than simply leaving the roof door open and hoping everybody would contribute equally.
