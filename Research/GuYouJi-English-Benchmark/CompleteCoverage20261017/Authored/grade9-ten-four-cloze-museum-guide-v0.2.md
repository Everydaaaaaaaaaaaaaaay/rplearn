### 全组回填

The West Lane Museum offers a small electronic guide. Visitors press a number to hear information about an object. A review changed its purchasing plan.

Staff expected visitors to want more recordings. Many instead reported difficulty finding the right number. The numbers on the objects were small, and the guide's buttons were close together. Adding stories would not remove those problems.

The museum tested a revised guide with twenty volunteers. Its larger buttons had spaces between them. The object labels also showed larger numbers. Volunteers had to find three objects and play matching information. Staff recorded whether they could do this independently or needed help.

Sixteen finished without assistance. Four still needed directions to the first room. The museum therefore improved the entrance sign as well. The results were not proof that every future visitor would manage alone. They showed which parts had improved and where support was still necessary.

The new recordings were not simply made longer. Repeated sentences were removed, while essential descriptions stayed. A printed text offered the same information for people who preferred reading.

The museum's purchasing decision was based on these observations. It chose clearer controls and labels before a larger recording library. The purpose was to help people reach the information they wanted.

This was a limited trial: the volunteers visited on one quiet morning, not during a crowded weekend. Staff will check the guide again under busier conditions. The report keeps that detail beside the positive results.

For the museum, a useful guide is judged by its performance in actual tasks. An advertisement saying 'every visitor will find information instantly' is a promise, not an observed result.
