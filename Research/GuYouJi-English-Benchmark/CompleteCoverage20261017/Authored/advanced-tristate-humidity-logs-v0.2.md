### Reconstructing an archive’s indoor climate

A research brief from the fictional Bellmere Archive describes an attempt to compare indoor humidity records collected in two different decades. The earlier measurements were written in notebooks by a caretaker using a portable analogue instrument. A later monitoring scheme installed digital sensors permanently beside selected shelves. Although both schemes recorded humidity, the researchers did not treat their numbers as directly interchangeable. They first examined calibration notes and excluded intervals during which either instrument was awaiting repair. The comparison concerned the remaining records, rather than every measurement that had originally been collected.

The project was financed by a grant from a local historical foundation. This paid for the transcription of the notebooks, the checking of shelf codes and a short period in which the old instrument and a modern sensor were operated side by side. Volunteers entered the handwritten numbers into a spreadsheet, while a second reader checked entries against photographs of the original pages. When a figure could not be read confidently, it was retained as an unresolved entry instead of being replaced with a plausible value. The grant report listed these procedures as separate expenditure categories.

For the final climate map, a usable observation had to carry both a date and a shelf code. A measurement without either identifier could still be mentioned in the archive’s narrative history, but it was not assigned a location on the map. The authors cautioned that an apparent difference between two periods might reflect changes in measurement practice as well as changes in the building itself. They therefore presented the map as a tool for deciding where to investigate next, rather than as proof that a particular storage policy had caused the observed pattern.
