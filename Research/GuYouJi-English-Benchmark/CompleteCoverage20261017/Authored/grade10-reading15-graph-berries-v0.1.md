### A组（第1—3题）

#### A1

Who should decide how much fruit is picked from a common berry plot? In an imaginary village, two groups agree that stripping the bushes once and abandoning them would be unacceptable. But they give different meanings to a successful plan. The wildlife group wants the plot to remain largely untouched, leaving fruit and shelter for other living things. It favours very little picking. The food group wants the largest harvest that can continue without using up the bushes' capacity to replace what is removed. It treats a repeated supply as a legitimate objective, rather than seeking the largest one-day collection.

A simplified model helps explain the second goal. The horizontal axis shows picking pressure, increasing from left to right. The vertical axis shows continuing harvest, increasing upward. There is one curve, with four labelled points. It does not show the weight of fruit left on the bushes. The labels refer to positions on the harvest curve, so their height must not be mistaken for a count of remaining bushes. The model is invented for this account; the picture contains no field measurements.

![An imaginary berry plot. Horizontal axis: Picking pressure, low to high. Vertical axis: Continuing berry harvest. A single smooth curve starts at zero at D, rises through C, reaches its only peak at A, and falls to zero at B. No stock curve or numerical measurements are shown.](build/english-benchmark-authority-20261017/grade10-reading15-graph-berries-curve-v0.1.svg)

The assumed replacement process explains why more pressure does not always produce a larger continuing harvest. When productive growth is very scarce, little fruit can be replaced. When growth is extremely crowded, competition also limits replacement. Between these extremes, replacement is greatest. Picking that replacement while preserving the productive base permits the largest continuing harvest in this model. Removing more than can be replaced would not keep the same productive base stable.

These assumptions would need checking before the model guided real picking. Weather, species differences and the needs of animals may alter the result. More basically, the wildlife group could still reject the food group's goal even if it accepted every feature of the curve. A diagram about harvest can clarify a disagreement; it cannot by itself decide which purpose a community ought to value most.

### B组（第4—7题）

#### B1

A Margin for Voices, the new memoir by Daren Moss, has the outward form of a book about a small film festival. There are screening rooms, late deliveries and arguments about the programme. Yet the real subject is how Daren changed his understanding of participation after he began using captions. As a reviewer, I found the backstage incidents most useful when they revealed that personal change, rather than when they simply listed successful screenings.

His earlier travel novel followed an invented reporter through noisy railway stations. In the memoir, he returns to actual memories from his own working life and checks them against festival letters. Several letters show that captions were once treated as an optional extra to arrange after everything else. Daren does not claim that every festival followed that practice. He uses records from one event to explain the assumptions he himself had accepted.

At first, he carried on attending meetings but spoke less and less. He describes being **on the sidelines** even while seated at the table: he could see people exchange ideas but often missed enough words to stop joining in. Later, written summaries and clearer turn-taking helped him contribute questions. The passage concerns exclusion from a shared discussion, not a change in the physical location of his chair or a recovery of hearing.

The book's first part moves slowly through programme notes and letters. Impatient readers might wish Daren had reached the meetings sooner. Still, those records show why a familiar working routine seemed difficult to question. In the later chapters, a volunteer who uses captions challenges his first attempts to redesign the event. Her criticism gives the story an important balance: receiving help did not make him an instant expert on everybody else's needs.

I recommend the memoir for this willingness to remain unfinished. Its achievement is a careful account of learning to make participation possible in one setting. It is neither a technical guide to captioning every film nor proof that one arrangement fits every person. The uneven pace matters less than the honesty of the changes the book describes.

### C组（第8—11题）

#### C1

Imagine a research workshop trying to obtain separate streams of water and reusable mineral from a prepared salt mixture. Its prototype, called the Fold Plate, is described in a fictional laboratory report. The team is interested in two related problems: collecting evaporated water and keeping the remaining mixture moving. A plate that works briefly and then becomes covered in solids would not meet those aims, however quickly it warmed at the beginning.

The workshop treats several dark ribs on a metal sheet. Small grooves on these ribs draw solution from a tray and spread it across the warmed surfaces. Sunlight supplies the heat for evaporation. Vapour meets the underside of a sloping clear lid, cools and runs into a separate water channel. The sheet's flat border is left untreated; the thicker liquid remaining after evaporation is directed there. The border receives that liquid rather than acting as another evaporation rib.

As water leaves the solution, the remaining material becomes more concentrated. If it dries across the ribs, it can obstruct the grooves supplying fresh solution. Open side passages carry it to the border before this happens. In a test using the same prepared mixture and light level, a version with these passages stays supplied longer than a version whose passages are closed. This comparison concerns movement during that test, not a guarantee that the plate never needs cleaning.

At the border, the team installs a removable woven insert with a special coating. The coating holds a selected mineral while other material passes into a collection vessel. The mineral can then be removed from the insert for a second use. This is the report's example of recovering value from what would otherwise remain a leftover stream. The coating is not described as a way to improve the colour or taste of the separate water stream.

The account carefully distinguishes a promising arrangement from a finished treatment system. The team has not tested every natural water source, established drinking-water quality or measured a year of outdoor operation. Its next questions concern cleaning the ribs and changing the insert. Readers should not turn a single laboratory comparison into a promise of safe household water.

### D组（第12—15题）

#### D1

The question in a fictional communication study sounds simple: what happens when children hear instructions during a busy activity? A first test presents the same recording to each child through one speaker. This helps the researchers keep the sound material constant. However, a recording played without other people cannot show how a child is invited into a conversation or asks for a message to be repeated. The team therefore adds observations in everyday clubs.

One club organises a lively shared model-building session. Its members say that doing things together is central to the club. The other divides projects into short, quiet pairs and joins the results at the end. Neither routine is treated as a national or ethnic characteristic. Families have chosen their clubs, so an observed difference might reflect who joined as well as how the session is arranged. This selection makes a simple causal ranking unsafe.

During the shared session, several speakers sometimes overlap. Observers also count frequent requests for children's ideas. In the paired session, individual sentences are generally easier to separate from background voices, but fewer requests for new ideas occur in the same observation time. The report lists these findings separately. It does not combine them into a single score for which club teaches language best, because learning over time has not been measured.

For someone who finds speech in background noise hard to follow, a social invitation may arrive with words that are difficult to access. Frequent invitations and clear speech are both relevant; one is not a substitute for the other. A child may want to contribute and still need a repeated or written instruction. Conversely, hearing a sentence clearly does not tell the observers whether the child has been offered a turn to respond.

The team proposes a trial in which the shared club keeps its group project but adds a visible instruction board and agreed pauses for questions. Members would help design the pauses so that the shared activity remains recognisable to them. The proposal still needs testing with the participating children. It is a plan for improving access within a valued routine, not an established hearing treatment or a demand that every club use quiet pairs.
