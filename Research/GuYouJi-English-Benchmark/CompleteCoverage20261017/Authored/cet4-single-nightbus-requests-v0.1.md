# 四级：夜间公交的临时下客点

### Passage 1

In the fictional town of Alwick, the last bus from the shopping district followed the same route as the daytime service. Passengers could predict exactly where it would stop. But several cleaners finishing work late had to walk back along a dark road after leaving it. The regular route passed their street without stopping because there was no marked bus stop there.

A drivers’ representative proposed allowing passengers to request an extra stop after nine in the evening. The bus would keep its usual route, but a driver could choose a safe place between official stops when somebody asked. Supporters thought this would shorten the final walk without requiring an additional service. The proposal did not promise a bus to every passenger’s door.

Some residents worried that repeated requests would make arrival times unreliable. Rather than introduce the arrangement everywhere, the council tried it on one evening route for a month. Drivers noted requests and any delays. They also recorded occasions when they could not stop safely, so a refusal would not be mistaken for forgetting a passenger.

The trial produced fewer requests than residents had feared, although a football match caused an unusually busy evening. Drivers wanted a clear rule about requests near road crossings. Passengers wanted the driver to explain a refusal before passing their street. Both points concerned how the arrangement should operate, rather than whether late workers deserved consideration.

The council continued the trial while developing clearer guidance. Its transport officer resisted describing the change as a replacement for proper stops or street lighting. Those improvements involved different decisions and remained necessary in some places. The request system offered a useful response to a particular difficulty, she said, but its success would depend on passengers understanding its limits and drivers having enough information to act consistently.
