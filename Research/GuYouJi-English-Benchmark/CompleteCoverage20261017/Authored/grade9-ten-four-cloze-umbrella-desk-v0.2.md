### 全组回填

At Millbridge School, a borrowing desk lends umbrellas to pupils who forget theirs. The school used to spend all its small budget on buying more umbrellas. Now it spends part of it on repair and other services. A survey helped explain the change.

Thirty regular users answered one question: what should the desk improve first? Eighteen chose the condition of the umbrellas, while twelve chose the number available. Several handles were loose, and two umbrellas would not stay open. More umbrellas alone would not solve these faults.

"It must stay open in the rain. I need something reliable, not just something new," one pupil wrote. The comment influenced the school's decisions. A local repair shop checked the damaged umbrellas. It could repair most of them for less than the cost of new ones. The desk bought replacements only for those beyond repair.

Pupils also wanted to know where to return an umbrella. A clear sign now names the desk and its opening hours. Each borrower accepts responsibility for bringing the item back by the agreed date. Helpers check the returned umbrellas regularly, so a new fault does not remain unnoticed for weeks.

These changes do not prove that every pupil in the town prefers repair. The figures come only from thirty regular users at one school. They do show why the desk changed its spending: most respondents wanted usable umbrellas first, because several existing ones were faulty.

The school will ask the same question next term. A new survey may show a different need. The desk keeps usable umbrellas; only those beyond repair are replaced by new ones.
