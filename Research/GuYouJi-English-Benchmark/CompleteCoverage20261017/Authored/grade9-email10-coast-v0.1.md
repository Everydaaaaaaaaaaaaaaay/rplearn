### B组（第6—10题）

#### B1

After a storm in a remote forest, a field team noticed something unexpected. One kind of large tree remained alive, although several nearby trees had died. Some climbing vines on its branches were dead too. The team began recording these cases rather than assuming that a strike had the same effect on every tree.

The researchers proposed an explanation. Electric current might move through connected branches and vines to nearby plants, instead of remaining in the large tree. This could reduce the damage to that tree. The report presented the idea as a possibility that needed testing, not an electrical process the observers had directly measured.

One tree in the records was easy to recognise. Its thick trunk rose well above the surrounding plants, and its broad crown spread over a gap in the forest. With fewer living vines and fewer nearby competitors, it had more access to light and space after the storm.

How old was it? The team could offer only an estimate. The wood they examined did not show a complete sequence of clear annual rings, so simply counting visible lines could not give an exact age for this individual. Its large size alone did not supply the missing information.

The account shows how a surprising event can lead to further questions about a forest. It describes observations and a proposed explanation, while leaving the exact process and the tree’s precise age unresolved.
