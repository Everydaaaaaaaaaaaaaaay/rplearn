## 学生题面

阅读短文，用英语填空或回答问题，注意各处词数要求。

Two classes at Hill School are planning a day outside. They have the same budget and the same amount of time, but they make their plans in different ways.

Class A uses an individual plan. Each student chooses one activity from a list before the trip. Some want to draw the old bridge; others want to visit the small garden. The teacher makes sure that each activity has an adult to lead it. During the trip, students follow their own activity groups and meet again at the bus. This gives them time to explore a personal interest, although classmates in different groups spend less time together.

Class B uses a shared plan. A trip leader first asks everyone what they would enjoy. The leader then suggests one place with several things to do, and the class discusses the choice. Students may not get their first choice, but they travel and take part in the main activity together. The leader says that the important part is listening to others before making a decision.

Both classes have to think about cost, travel time and the weather. An individual plan does not mean that students can go anywhere without an adult. A shared plan does not mean that everyone has the same interests. The teachers say that a good plan should explain how the activities will work, rather than simply promise that everybody will be happy.
