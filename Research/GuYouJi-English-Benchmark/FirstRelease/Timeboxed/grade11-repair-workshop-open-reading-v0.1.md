## 学生题面

阅读短文，用英语按各题词数要求回答。

I arrived early for a community repair workshop, carrying a small wooden box with a loose lid. Near the doorway stood a boy about my age. He was holding a broken model boat and reading the list of activities on the wall. Each time somebody approached the entrance, he stepped aside, as if he did not want to be in the way. His father waited a little further down the corridor.

The room was busy. Experienced members were choosing tools, while newcomers searched for places to sit. The boy looked **bewildered**: he turned the boat over several times, glanced at the different tables, and then looked back towards the exit. I was about to suggest a seat when a young woman named Mei came over. She did not ask why he was still standing outside. Instead, she asked whether he could help her sort a tray of small wooden pieces.

At first, he said that he knew very little about repairs. Mei explained that she did not need an expert. She needed someone who could compare the shapes and put matching pieces together. She cleared a space beside her and placed the tray between them. The boy put down his boat and began. After a few minutes, he pointed out that one piece had been placed in the wrong group. Mei thanked him and asked him to show the others how he had noticed it.

Soon, two people at the same table were looking at his model boat with him. One asked about the missing part; the other helped him draw its shape on paper. His father came into the room and sat nearby, but the boy continued talking to the group. When I finally reached a table with my own box, I could hear him asking where to find a ruler.

Later, I asked Mei why she had given him a job instead of simply telling him which table to join. “Sometimes an invitation works better when a person has a reason to accept it,” she said. I suspect that she also wanted the others to see him as someone who could contribute, rather than as a newcomer waiting for help.

I left with a firmer lid on my box and a different idea of welcome. A friendly room needs more than an open door. It needs people who notice who is still standing outside and find a way to bring them into the conversation.
