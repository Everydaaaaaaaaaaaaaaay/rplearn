## Should Every School Art Display Give a First Prize?

At a school meeting, students discuss whether every art display needs a first prize. Everyone agrees that students should have a chance to show their work, but they disagree about how the display should end.

### The case for a first prize

Supporters say that a prize gives the event a clear goal. Students know that a panel will choose one piece and explain its choice. Preparing for this result may encourage them to plan their work carefully.

They also argue that winning can build confidence. A student who rarely speaks in class might feel proud when a painting receives attention. Of course, supporters do not claim that one result measures a person's whole ability. For them, the prize is one new challenge, rather than a final judgment. They would allow the same student to enter again with a different piece next term.

### The case against a first prize

Opponents worry that competition may become the centre of the event. Some students could spend more time comparing the chances of winning than discussing the ideas in the pictures. A display without a first prize could keep attention on what the artists wanted to express.

They suggest asking students to compare their new work with an earlier piece. In this way, students can see their own progress, even when other people are working on very different subjects. They also want every participant to receive useful feedback, not just a winner's name on a board. A comment about colour or composition could give a student something specific to try next time.

The meeting ends without one final rule. The students decide that the next display should explain its purpose before anyone enters.
