## 学生题面

阅读六项活动介绍，根据每个人的具体需求选择最合适的活动。

The following six weekend activities take place at an imaginary youth centre.

### 共享候选

A. River Path Morning: On Sunday mornings, a guide takes students along the riverside path to watch water birds and photograph plants. The walk stays outdoors and includes quiet stops for observation. It is not a sports competition or an indoor history talk.

B. Makers' Gift Table: On Saturday afternoons, students use clean old paper and small wooden pieces to make picture frames. A tutor shows simple techniques, and everyone can take their finished frame home as a gift. No long outdoor walk is included.

C. Court Together: On Sunday afternoons, students learn basic volleyball skills and play short team games. Groups change during the session so that participants meet different teammates. Beginners are welcome, but the main activity is exercise rather than watching a film.

D. Voices of the Town: On Sunday afternoons, older residents bring photographs of local streets and explain how daily life used to be different. Students can ask questions and compare the pictures with a map. This is an indoor discussion, not a long outdoor tour.

E. Round-table English: On Saturday mornings, students discuss a short English story in pairs and then in small groups. A leader gives each person time to speak and helps with useful expressions. The meeting is intended for active speaking, rather than silent film watching.

F. Screen Stories: On Saturday afternoons, a host introduces a film and then shows it in a quiet hall. After a short break, visitors can read information about how it was made. There is no craft activity, team sport or organised speaking practice.
