## 学生题面

阅读短文，用英语按各题词数要求回答。

WindowWalk began as a small museum programme for people who could not travel to the building. A guide carries a camera through selected galleries while a remote group watches the live view. Visitors can ask the guide to stop, move closer to an object, or answer a question. They choose the route from a short list before the session begins.

Affordable cameras and a shared booking platform have helped the programme move beyond its first trial. Previously, each visit required a technician to arrange a separate connection. Now the guides can use the same equipment and booking system for several different groups. A session still needs preparation, but setting up the connection takes much less time.

One visitor, Elena, joined a tour from her kitchen while her young son was resting in the next room. She liked being able to ask questions without arranging a long journey and childcare for the whole afternoon. However, she noticed that the screen could not **replicate** the experience of standing beside a large painting. Its size and texture were difficult to judge, even when the camera moved closer. She called the tour an additional way to visit, rather than a complete replacement.

The programme has limits of its own. Only selected galleries are included, and visitors cannot ask the guide to enter a closed room. If several people speak at once, the guide asks them to take turns. The museum also tells participants in advance when a connection problem may require a new appointment.

Although more groups have requested sessions, expansion has been slow because too few trained guides are available. Knowing the collection is only part of the job. A guide also has to handle the camera while following questions from people who are not in the room. The museum plans to train more guides before adding further sessions.

For Elena, the main value is choice. She still hopes to see the paintings in person one day, but the remote tour has made a conversation with the museum possible much sooner.
