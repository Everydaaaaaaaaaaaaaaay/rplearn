阅读文章，从每题四个选项中选出最佳选项。

<!-- passage:start -->
At Northbank School, where courtyard seats were exposed to direct sunlight, students often avoided them at lunchtime, leading the student council to suggest a light roof above one seating area. Before asking the school to pay for it, a small team wanted to find out whether shade would make the area more useful.

For one week, the team placed a temporary cloth cover above six seats, counting how many were occupied at twelve thirty each day and asking users whether they would return. The numbers looked encouraging because more students sat there than in the previous week, while several users also said they preferred the covered seats.

However, science teacher Ms Ray asked what else had changed between the two weeks that might help explain the increase. A popular lunchtime club had moved to a room beside the courtyard, where students waiting for it often used the seats, and the weather had also been cooler. Either change might have affected the count, which showed that the covered area was being used without establishing how much of the increase had been caused by shade.

For their next test, the team used two areas with the same number of seats, one of which had a cover while the other did not, recording both at the same time over several days. They noted weather and nearby activities and asked students why they chose an area, recognising that this comparison, though unable to remove every possible difference, would provide a clearer picture than comparing different weeks.

The council then revised its report, retaining the positive comments while adding the limits of the first test and explaining what further evidence was needed. "We still think a roof is worth considering," said council member Ella. "But a recommendation is stronger when readers can see what it is based on, including what we haven't established yet."
<!-- passage:end -->

1. Why did the team carry out the first test?
   A. To choose a new room for the lunchtime club.
   B. To learn whether shade would make the seating area more useful.
   C. To find the cheapest cloth sold near the school.
   D. To compare students' opinions of different teachers.
2. What did Ms Ray point out about the first test?
   A. Its users had refused to give any comments.
   B. The team had counted seats at different times every day.
   C. The school had already decided to build a permanent roof.
   D. Other changes might explain part of the increase in seat use.
3. How would the next test improve the comparison?
   A. By recording covered and uncovered areas during the same period.
   B. By asking only the students who liked the first cover.
   C. By cancelling every lunchtime activity at the school.
   D. By treating weather as the only possible cause of seat use.
4. What can we learn from the council's revised report?
   A. A recommendation should hide uncertain evidence.
   B. Positive comments are enough to prove a single cause.
   C. Useful recommendations can state both support and limits.
   D. Further testing is unnecessary when an idea is popular.
