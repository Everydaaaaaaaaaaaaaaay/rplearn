# 高一原创四题阅读候选：校园遮阴试验

虚构校内报告，参照[规格0.2](../Specifications/grade10-v0.2.md)和g10.2024.readingD完整四题四选说明模式。以下记录不声称真实科学研究；题面不显示可直接泄露主旨的标题。

阅读文章，从每题四个选项中选出最佳选项。

<!-- passage:start -->
At Northbank School, students often avoided the courtyard at lunchtime because its seats were in direct sunlight. The student council suggested putting a light roof above one seating area. Before asking the school to pay for it, a small team wanted to find out whether shade would make that area more useful.

The team placed a temporary cloth cover above six seats for one week. They counted how many seats were occupied at twelve thirty each day and asked users whether they would return. The numbers looked encouraging: more students sat there than in the previous week. Several users also said they preferred the covered seats.

However, science teacher Ms Ray asked what else had changed. During the second week, a popular lunchtime club had moved to a room beside the courtyard. Students waiting for the club often used the seats. The weather had also been cooler. Either change might have affected the count. The results showed that the covered area was being used, but they did not show how much of the increase was caused by shade.

For their next test, the team used two seating areas with the same number of seats. One had a cover and the other did not. They recorded both at the same time over several days and noted changes in weather and nearby activities. They also asked students why they chose a particular area. This comparison would not remove every possible difference, but it would provide a clearer picture than comparing two different weeks.

The council then revised its report. It kept the positive comments, added the limits of the first test and explained what further evidence was needed. "We still think a roof is worth considering," said council member Ella. "But a recommendation is stronger when readers can see what it is based on, including what we haven't established yet."
<!-- passage:end -->

1. Why did the team carry out the first test?
   A. To choose a new room for the lunchtime club.
   B. To learn whether shade would make the seating area more useful.
   C. To find the cheapest cloth sold near the school.
   D. To compare students' opinions of different teachers.
2. What did Ms Ray point out about the first test?
   A. Its users had refused to give any comments.
   B. The team had counted seats at different times every day.
   C. The school had already decided to build a permanent roof.
   D. Other changes might explain part of the increase in seat use.
3. How would the next test improve the comparison?
   A. By recording covered and uncovered areas during the same period.
   B. By asking only the students who liked the first cover.
   C. By cancelling every lunchtime activity at the school.
   D. By treating weather as the only possible cause of seat use.
4. What can we learn from the council's revised report?
   A. A recommendation should hide uncertain evidence.
   B. Positive comments are enough to prove a single cause.
   C. Useful recommendations can state both support and limits.
   D. Further testing is unnecessary when an idea is popular.
