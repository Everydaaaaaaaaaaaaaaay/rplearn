# 高三原创论证阅读候选：服务统计的边界

虚构社区项目，参照[规格0.2](../Specifications/grade12-v0.2.md)与g12.2025anshan.readingC的概念/例证/论证四题四选结构。源分母/标题风险作为反例，不沿用源键。题面不展示候选标题。

阅读文章，从每题A、B、C、D四个选项中选出最佳选项。

<!-- passage:start -->
When the Willow Tool Bank introduced an online booking service, its monthly report seemed impressive. Every confirmed booking had resulted in a successful loan. The manager described this as a "perfect completion rate" and proposed replacing the old telephone service. Yet several residents told the local committee that the new system had made borrowing harder.

The disagreement was not necessarily a dispute about the accuracy of the recorded loans. The report counted only requests that had become confirmed bookings. Residents who could not find an available time, failed to complete the online form or gave up before confirmation left no entry in that total. A flawless record within the measured category could coexist with difficulties outside it.

Consider two imaginary months. In the first, twenty residents requested a tool and all twenty obtained it. In the second, forty residents tried, but only twenty reached the confirmation stage; those twenty all obtained their tools. Both months would produce a perfect completion rate under the manager's definition. However, the second month would leave twenty unsuccessful attempts unrepresented. The rate answered a narrow question—whether confirmed bookings were fulfilled—while the committee wanted to know whether residents could obtain the service they needed.

This does not make the original measure useless. Staff need to know whether they can honour bookings they accept. It does mean that the measure cannot, by itself, justify a decision about every resident's access. Nor can an absence of recorded complaints establish that nobody had trouble: a person unable to use the booking form might also be unable to reach its feedback page.

The committee asked for a revised report combining completed loans with records of unsuccessful requests and interviews with telephone users. It also advised keeping a phone option during the next trial. Even then, the report would require limits: interviews might miss people who never contacted the service, and attempted requests would need a consistent definition. Better evaluation would not replace one reassuring number with another. It would make the relationship between the question, the evidence and the people represented in it easier to inspect.
<!-- passage:end -->

1. Why could the perfect completion rate coexist with residents' difficulties?
   A. It counted every resident who had considered borrowing.
   B. It excluded attempts that never became confirmed bookings.
   C. It treated fulfilled bookings as unsuccessful loans.
   D. It included all telephone requests from the previous year.
2. What is the main purpose of the two imaginary months?
   A. To explain how to repair a damaged borrowed tool.
   B. To prove that borrowing demand always doubles each month.
   C. To compare the prices of online and telephone services.
   D. To show that the same reported rate can hide different access outcomes.
3. What is the writer's view of the original measure?
   A. It serves a limited purpose but cannot settle the broader access question alone.
   B. It must be false because some residents disliked the new system.
   C. It proves that residents without bookings did not need tools.
   D. It should replace interviews because recorded numbers are always sufficient.
4. Which title best covers the text?
   A. Why Telephone Services Must Always Replace Online Forms
   B. A Complete Guide to Repairing Community Tools
   C. A Perfect Rate and the Requests It Leaves Out
   D. How to Eliminate Every Uncertainty in a Monthly Report
