# 高二原创论证阅读候选：Keeping Projects Going

参照[高二规格0.2](../Specifications/grade11-v0.2.md)及g11.2025.readingD的多原因区分/论证转入建议结构。学生委员会为假设情境，观点由本篇作者提出，未虚构专家研究或学生试测。完整四题四选及逐项作者解答。

## 学生题面

阅读短文，从每题所给的四个选项中选出最佳选项。

<!-- passage:start -->
Imagine a student committee planning an arts festival. A new stage design attracts enthusiastic discussion, while a weekly equipment-loan desk is barely mentioned. Yet the festival depends on instruments being available and returned in good condition. A project can be easy to overlook without being unimportant. Before accepting another attractive proposal, the committee needs to understand why its members are already carrying so much work.

One difficulty is estimating the continuing cost of a project. Planning a first event is relatively concrete: there is a date, a room and a list of tasks. Keeping a service running through a term is less clear. Members may count the opening day but forget the hours needed to replace damaged items, answer questions or train new volunteers. The trouble is not necessarily unwillingness to work. It may be an incomplete picture of what the promise requires.

A second difficulty concerns attention. A new project offers a moment when everyone can see what has been achieved. Maintaining an existing service rarely receives the same applause. If decisions are based only on what can be presented at the next meeting, useful work that happens quietly may lose support. The committee might then launch an impressive new activity while allowing the service that makes it possible to weaken.

There is also a different reason for continuing a demanding project. Some members know that other students depend on it. They may understand its cost perfectly and receive little public praise, but still feel responsible for the users. Their decision cannot simply be described as poor planning or a desire for attention. Changing such a project requires considering what will happen to the people who rely on it.

These differences matter. A committee cannot solve every difficulty by making a more exciting presentation, or by telling volunteers to try harder. Nor does recognising limits mean that all new ideas should be rejected. What it needs is a practical way to examine its promises before adding another one.
<!-- passage:end -->

1. Why does the writer mention the equipment-loan desk?

   A. To show that organising new festivals is always wasteful.
   B. To show that little-noticed routine work can still be essential.
   C. To explain how the committee should buy new instruments.
   D. To suggest that only trained volunteers can join the committee.

2. What problem is described in Paragraph 2?

   A. Members may fail to include the continuing work in their plans.
   B. Members may refuse every task after the opening event.
   C. Members may disagree about which room is the most attractive.
   D. Members may receive too much praise for an existing service.

3. According to Paragraph 4, why might some members continue a demanding project?

   A. They cannot estimate how much time it takes.
   B. They expect it to attract the most public attention.
   C. They have no interest in proposing new activities.
   D. They feel responsible for the people who use it.

4. What would the next paragraph most probably discuss?

   A. The history of stage designs at school festivals.
   B. Why every new proposal should be accepted immediately.
   C. A method for reviewing the committee's commitments.
   D. Ways of making routine work unnecessary at school.

## 答案与实际作者解答

答案：B/A/D/C。
