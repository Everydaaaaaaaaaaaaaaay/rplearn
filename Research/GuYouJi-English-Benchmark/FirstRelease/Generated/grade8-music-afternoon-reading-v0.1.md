# 初二原创成长叙事：At the Welcome Desk

虚构社区音乐活动，依据[规格0.2](../Specifications/grade8-v0.2.md)和完整配套g8.2025fengtai.readingB的成长叙事/三题四选模式。当前午餐说明阅读不代表本体裁，新增独立故事家族，不把旧题换标题计新样本。

阅读短文，从每题所给的四个选项中选出最佳选项。

<!-- passage:start -->
Last month, I agreed to help at a community music afternoon. My friend Sara had volunteered there before, and she made the job sound easy. But when I was asked to work at the welcome desk, I began to worry. I knew very little about music. What if a visitor asked a question I could not answer?

The night before the event, I studied the programme and tried to remember every musician's name. I even wrote a long welcome speech. When the first visitors arrived, I said it as quickly as I could. One man waited until I had finished and then asked where his daughter should leave her violin case. My speech had not answered his question at all.

Sara noticed what had happened. She did not tell me to learn more names. Instead, she suggested asking each visitor what they needed before giving directions. She also showed me a list of helpers who could answer different questions. I did not have to know everything, but I had to know whom to ask.

Soon a woman came to the desk carrying two bags. I began to point towards the concert room, but she explained that she was bringing materials for the children's activity upstairs. This time, I listened to her whole question. Then I called the activity helper, who came down to meet her. The woman thanked us for making her first visit easier.

Later, several families arrived together, and a line formed outside the door. I felt nervous again. However, I asked Sara to give out programmes while I helped people with their questions. We worked as a team instead of trying to do every task at the same time.

At the end of the afternoon, the organiser thanked all the volunteers. I had not learned every musician's name, and I still needed help with some questions. Yet I no longer thought that being useful meant having every answer ready. It meant paying attention, finding the right person to help, and speaking up when I needed support. I asked whether I could return for the next event.
<!-- passage:end -->

1. Why did the writer worry about working at the welcome desk?

   A. Sara had refused to help at the event.
   B. The writer was expected to perform with the musicians.
   C. The writer feared being unable to answer visitors' questions.
   D. The organiser had asked the writer to carry all the materials upstairs.

2. What helped the woman with two bags reach the right helper?

   A. The writer listened to her whole question before calling someone.
   B. The writer repeated the welcome speech more slowly.
   C. Sara told her to leave her bags in the concert room.
   D. The organiser introduced every musician to her.

3. What did the writer learn from the volunteer experience?

   A. A volunteer should remember every name before offering help.
   B. Visitors usually need the same kind of information.
   C. Asking another helper shows that someone is unsuitable for the job.
   D. Listening and seeking support can help someone become useful.

## 作者逐题依据与干扰项检查

| 题 | 正解依据 | 各干扰项与范围核对 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 C | 第一段little about music、担心无法回答访客问题，第二段提前记名/写稿是其应对 | A Sara有经验并在场协助；B工作为接待非演出，不因music活动推出演奏任务；D搬材料的是第四段来访女性，不能换成组织者给叙述者的任务。 |
| 2 A | 第四段听完整问题后联系activity helper，对方下楼接来访者 | B长欢迎词发生在第二段给男性客人的第一次尝试，没有对女性再次念稿；C叙述者原指concert room但听后修正，不能把未完成方向归给Sara；D组织者只在末段致谢，没有给此访客逐个介绍音乐家。 |
| 3 D | 末段明示paying attention/finding the right person/speaking up，前面的询问与分工支撑这一反思 | A末段尚未记全名字仍能帮忙，否定必要条件；B男性要放琴盒、女性要找活动、家庭排队，需求各异；C作者主动求助并愿意返回，全文未把需要支援视为不适合。 |

作者完整解题C/A/D，逐项检查说话者/人物、首次尝试/后续改变、原拟路线/实际接应和整体成长。第2只问这一女性得到帮助的过程，不扩大为所有访客都由同一方式解决；第3不把掌握音乐知识写成毫无作用，而是排除必须拥有每个答案的过强条件。三题分别考初始原因、具体行动和整体意义，不要求读者了解音乐专业知识。
