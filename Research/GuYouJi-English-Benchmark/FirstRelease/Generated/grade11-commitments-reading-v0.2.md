## 学生题面

阅读短文，从每题所给的四个选项中选出最佳选项。

<!-- passage:start -->
Imagine a student committee whose members, while enthusiastically discussing a new stage design, barely mention the equipment-loan desk that operates every week. Although the desk attracts little attention, the festival depends on instruments being available and returned in good condition. Before accepting another proposal that promises an exciting event, the committee needs to understand why work that can easily be overlooked still matters, and why its members are already carrying so much of it.

One difficulty is estimating the continuing cost of a project, which is less obvious than the date, room and task list needed for its first event. Members who count only the opening day may forget the hours required to replace damaged items, answer questions or train new volunteers. What appears to be unwillingness to work may therefore reflect an incomplete picture of what the original promise actually requires.

A second difficulty concerns attention: whereas a new project offers a moment when everyone can see what has been achieved, maintaining an existing service rarely receives the same applause. If decisions depend only on what can be presented at the next meeting, the useful work happening quietly between meetings may lose support. The committee might then launch an impressive activity while allowing the service on which that activity depends to weaken.

There is also a different reason for continuing a demanding project, one that cannot simply be explained by poor planning or a desire for attention. Some members, knowing that other students depend on the service, understand its cost perfectly yet still feel responsible for its users, even when their work receives little public praise. Changing such a project requires considering what will happen to those who rely on it, rather than assuming that everybody who accepts a heavy workload has failed to think it through.

These differences matter because a committee cannot solve every difficulty either by making a more exciting presentation or by telling volunteers to try harder. Nor does recognising limits mean that all new ideas should be rejected. What is needed is a practical way of examining the committee's promises before another one is added to them.
<!-- passage:end -->

1. Why does the writer mention the equipment-loan desk?

   A. To show that organising new festivals is always wasteful.
   B. To show that little-noticed routine work can still be essential.
   C. To explain how the committee should buy new instruments.
   D. To suggest that only trained volunteers can join the committee.

2. What problem is described in Paragraph 2?

   A. Members may fail to include the continuing work in their plans.
   B. Members may refuse every task after the opening event.
   C. Members may disagree about which room is the most attractive.
   D. Members may receive too much praise for an existing service.

3. According to Paragraph 4, why might some members continue a demanding project?

   A. They cannot estimate how much time it takes.
   B. They expect it to attract the most public attention.
   C. They have no interest in proposing new activities.
   D. They feel responsible for the people who use it.

4. What would the next paragraph most probably discuss?

   A. The history of stage designs at school festivals.
   B. Why every new proposal should be accepted immediately.
   C. A method for reviewing the committee's commitments.
   D. Ways of making routine work unnecessary at school.
