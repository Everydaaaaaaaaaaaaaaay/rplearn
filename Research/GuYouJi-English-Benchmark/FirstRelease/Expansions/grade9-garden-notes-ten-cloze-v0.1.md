完整参考回填：

Nina joined a group preparing a display about the school garden. She had a clear plan: draw a beautiful map and add the names of the plants. Her teacher first asked her to check the labels beside the pots. Nina thought this was a boring task and wanted to start drawing at once.

She took a map from a cupboard, but some pots were in different positions from those shown on it. She wondered whether someone had moved them that morning. Then she noticed the date. The old map was marked 2 May, while the new plan on the teacher's desk was marked 9 May. The map she was using was older than the latest plan. The teacher explained that the garden layout had changed during the previous week.

Nina decided to check the plant names against the labels in the current garden before drawing. Two classmates noticed a sign hidden by leaves. After moving the leaves aside, they could read its name clearly. Some other labels were damaged, so the group left those names blank rather than guess. A garden worker later supplied the missing information.

The finished display was not just attractive; its names were accurate. Nina now understood why her teacher had asked for careful records. A good-looking map could still be misleading if it described last week's garden instead of the one visitors would see.
