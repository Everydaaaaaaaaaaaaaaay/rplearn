### Which Statement Are We Following?

The school maker club lends cameras to students for small projects. On its notice board, there is a rule, a guideline and an agreement. Some members call all three "rules" and think they work in the same way. The club leader uses one borrowing story to explain why the difference matters. Knowing the difference can help members understand what is expected before they act.

In this club, a rule is binding: every borrower must write their name in the loan book before taking a camera. Nobody can skip this step just because they are in a hurry. A guideline is different. It suggests returning equipment before the club session ends, but members can ask to use it for longer. An agreement is a plan that particular people accept together. It records what those people have promised to do, rather than setting the same plan for everyone.

On Monday, Noah wrote his name in the loan book and borrowed a camera. He and Mira agreed that he would return it at four, because she needed it for her own project. Later, Noah wanted to keep filming. He pointed to the guideline and said that using the camera for longer was allowed. However, the guideline had not changed the agreement he had made with Mira.

The leader asked Noah to speak to Mira instead of treating the guideline as permission from her. If they both accepted a new return time, they could change their agreement together. If not, Noah would need another way to finish filming. He could still follow the club's rule about recording loans while discussing a different plan with Mira. Different statements could apply to the same camera without replacing one another.

To avoid similar confusion, members now check three things before borrowing: what everyone must do, what the club suggests, and what they have agreed with another person. They also write down who will do what and when. The aim is to make expectations clear, not to fill the notice board with more words. Clear statements help members share equipment and make plans that others can understand.

A. easy to forget
B. useful only to two people
C. required to be followed
D. different for every borrower

2. Which statement about Monday's borrowing is supported by the passage?

A. The club requires every camera to be returned at four.
B. Writing a name in the loan book cancels a return agreement.
C. The guideline lets Noah change his agreement without asking Mira.
D. Noah and Mira can choose a new return time if both accept it.

3. What is the writer's main purpose in writing this passage?

A. To explain why students should always return equipment as early as possible.
B. To show how different kinds of statements help members understand expectations.
C. To compare the advantages of using a camera alone and with a partner.
D. To suggest replacing club rules with promises between individual members.
