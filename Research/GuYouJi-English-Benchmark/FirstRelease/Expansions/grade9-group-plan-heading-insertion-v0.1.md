### Making a Group Plan Work

A group may have plenty of ideas and still find it difficult to finish a project. One person starts making a poster while another thinks the group is preparing a talk. Members can work hard without working towards the same result. How can a group avoid this kind of confusion? __1__

#### Make the goal clear.

Before choosing colours or dividing jobs, decide what the finished project needs to do. Does it explain an idea, show a result or invite people to an event? When you offer a suggestion, remind yourself: "__2__" Then ask whether your suggestion would help the group reach that goal. An attractive idea is not necessarily a useful one for this particular project.

#### Check where people are.

Knowing who has a job is different from knowing how the job is going. Some members may have finished, while others are still waiting for materials. __3__ With this information, the group can offer help before a small delay affects everyone's work. Asking about progress should make problems visible, rather than make someone afraid to admit that they need help.

#### Leave room for changes.

A plan that worked last week may no longer fit the time or materials available now. __4__ This allows the group to review an earlier choice before it creates a bigger problem. A change should have a clear reason, however; replacing every decision at each meeting can stop the work from moving forward.

#### __5__

Memory is not always enough when several parts of a plan change. Two people may remember the same discussion differently. A shared note can record what the group decided, who will do each job and when to check it again. Members should be able to find the latest version, so they do not follow an older plan by mistake.

A useful plan connects the group's goal, current progress and next actions. It gives people something they can check and improve together.

A. Here are some ways to make the whole plan clearer.
B. If one member finishes early, the whole project is complete.
C. Set aside a little time to discuss new problems together.
D. We need to agree on what the project must achieve.
E. Write down what has been agreed.
F. A short update from everyone can show what help is needed.
G. No changes should be made after the first meeting.
