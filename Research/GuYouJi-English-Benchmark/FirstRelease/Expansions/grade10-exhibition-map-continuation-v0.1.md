# 高一读后续写：The Missing Route

参照温州2025高一期中第67题的“阅读前文＋两个给定段首＋约150词续写两段”形式。以下故事、两个段首、作者作答和任务化评分描述均为本次原创，人物与事件虚构。参考源的前文不作本题改写底稿，源范文也不作唯一内容路径。此稿是作者准备材料，尚无非作者或目标学生验证。

### 阅读前文

Mei and Daniel were making a visitor map for their school's Saturday exhibition. Mei drew the rooms and paths, while Daniel checked the signs. They had agreed to include a route without steps, so that visitors who could not use the stairs could reach the main hall. Their teacher wanted the final map on Friday morning.

On Thursday afternoon, Mei found Daniel pinning a new map to the classroom wall. It looked cleaner than her draft, but the route without steps was gone. A bright arrow now pointed everyone towards the front stairs. "You changed it without asking me," she said. Daniel explained that the back path took longer and made the map crowded. Mei tried to remind him of their agreement, but he was already talking about the colours.

"Then put your name on it," Mei said. She closed her notebook and walked away. Daniel called after her, but she did not turn round. She had spent two afternoons measuring that path. Now she felt that neither the work nor the people who needed it mattered to him.

At home, Mei opened the notebook again. Between two pages was a small drawing made by a visitor at last year's exhibition. The visitor had shown her where the stairs had stopped him. Mei had promised herself that this year's map would help. She wanted Daniel to understand, yet she was afraid that another conversation would end in another argument.

That evening, her phone lit up. Daniel had sent a photo of the new map with a question: "Could we meet in the classroom before the others arrive tomorrow? I think we need to talk." Mei looked at the drawing, then at the message. The map was still due the next morning.

## 作者区

### 完整作者示例（未评分）

Before the others arrived, Mei placed her notebook beside Daniel's map. She showed him the visitor's drawing and explained why the longer route mattered. Daniel studied it in silence. "I thought a simple map would be easier to use," he said, "but I left some visitors without a way in." Mei admitted that walking away had not helped him understand. Together, they marked the route in blue and added a small key. They checked that both routes could be followed from the entrance.

When their teacher came in, the two students were ready to explain their decision. Daniel described the problem with his first version, and Mei showed how the new key kept the map clear. Their teacher asked them to check the signs once more before printing. As they walked along the back path, Daniel carried the map while Mei checked each turn. The extra work felt different now. They were no longer defending separate designs; they were making one useful map together.

### 流畅但与前文矛盾的作者反例（未评分）

Before the others arrived, Mei placed her notebook beside Daniel's map. She thanked him for keeping the route without steps, exactly as they had agreed. Neither of them had ever disagreed about the design. The notebook contained only a list of prizes they hoped to win. They decided to remove all the arrows because every visitor already knew the school. Daniel smiled and said that there was no need to check the paths. Mei put away the notebook, pleased that the work had been so easy.

When their teacher came in, the two students were ready to explain their decision. They announced that the exhibition had already finished the previous evening. Every visitor had reached every room, so the map was no longer needed. Their teacher gave them a medal for their perfect teamwork. Mei and Daniel then left for a holiday, promising to make another map next year. They felt proud of their achievement and spent the rest of the morning discussing where to display the medal.

它保留两段首且语言连贯，却反写路线被删、争执和笔记本证据，另把尚待周六举行的展览变成已结束；因此“段首没改＋流畅”不能自动放行。此作答仍在尝试续写地图故事，**不是因事实矛盾就判全文完全无关或自动零分**。具体档/分待非作者按完整评分协议裁决，不能把作者反例当真人低分gold。
