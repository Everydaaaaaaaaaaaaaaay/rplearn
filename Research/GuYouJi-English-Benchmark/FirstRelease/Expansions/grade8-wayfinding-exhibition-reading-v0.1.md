### Finding the Right Door

When visitors get lost in a large building, people sometimes blame them for not paying attention. But a sign can be hard to use even when every word on it is correct. A sign pointing to "Study Space" may seem clear to a student who uses that room each week. A visitor looking for the school library may not know that the two names mean the same place.

Some signs use names familiar only to regular users. Others were put up before rooms moved, so the information on them is out of date. Signs can also be placed where people do not notice them until they have walked past the turn. In such cases, telling a visitor to "read more carefully" does not solve the whole problem.

Students from Eastfield School created an exhibition called Finding the Right Door. They collected examples of unclear signs and made improved versions. Visitors can read both versions and explain which would help them more. The exhibition will run for three days. Its creators want **it** to help visitors compare the old signs with the new ones.

"We want students to notice what information a first-time visitor needs," says Keira, one of the organisers. "Larger letters can help people see a sign, but they cannot correct an old room name. Listening to different visitors helps us find out what needs to change." The exhibition asks people to think about directions from another person's point of view.

1. Why might "Study Space" confuse the visitor in Paragraph 1?
   A. Every word on the sign is spelt incorrectly.
   B. The room is no longer used by students.
   C. The visitor may not know that it means the library.
2. What does Paragraph 2 mainly explain?
   A. What makes newcomers careless.
   B. Several reasons signs may confuse visitors.
   C. How all the room names were changed.
3. What does the word "it" in Paragraph 3 refer to?
   A. The exhibition.
   B. One old sign.
   C. The library.
4. Why did the writer write the passage?
   A. To prove regular users remember every room name.
   B. To help people make directions useful for different visitors.
   C. To encourage visitors to remember the old names.
