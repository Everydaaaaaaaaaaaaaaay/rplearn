### Working Together in a School Club

A school club does not need a large room or expensive equipment to be useful. What it needs is a way for members to work together regularly. **[1]** The following ideas can help a new club make a good start.

#### Keep the plan visible.

When a group agrees to make a short film, write down who will bring the camera and who will prepare the story. **[2]** A member who misses a meeting can then check the page rather than ask everyone to repeat the decision.

#### Share more than the finished work.

At a meeting, someone may show a poster and simply say, "I made this." Other members may admire the colours but learn little about the process. **[3]** Questions like these let people understand choices, and they can offer more useful suggestions.

#### Make mistakes easy to report.

A member who deletes a sound recording by accident may be tempted to hide it. The club leader has a choice about how to reply. **[4]** That response tells the member that honesty is welcome, even when the news is inconvenient. It makes it more likely that the group will hear about the problem while there is still time to record again.

#### **[5]**

Some problems need help from outside the group. A science teacher may show members how to use a sensor safely, while a member of the school media team may explain how to borrow a microphone. Explain what you are trying to do and ask a specific question. Asking for help is different from expecting someone else to finish the project for you.

A. Thank the member for explaining the problem before discussing a solution.
B. Put this list on a page that every member can read.
C. Colourful posters can attract attention before a club holds its first meeting.
D. A few clear routines can make that cooperation easier.
E. Invite more students.
F. Ask for specific help.
G. Ask what the maker changed and which idea did not work.
