### Checking the Garden Map

Leila joined her school's garden team in September. At first, she thought the hardest job would be growing the plants. Then she discovered a different problem: helping other people know what was growing where.

After a week of heavy rain, mud covered the words on several plant labels. A younger student wanted to water the mint, but could not read its label. Leila found the plant by using a map that the team had drawn earlier. However, when another student asked about a different plant, the map did not help. The team had moved that plant to a new bed without changing the map.

Leila realised that having information was not enough. It also had to be readable and up to date. With the team, she cleaned the labels and corrected the map. She did not make a new rule that every student must remember every plant's name. Instead, she wanted the garden's information to help people who were visiting for the first time.

Now, every Friday, Leila checks whether the words on each label can still be read and whether the map shows the plants in their current places. If something has changed, she tells the team so they can update the information together. Other students share this job when she is away.

"The check does not make our garden perfect," Leila says. "But it gives us a chance to find small problems before they confuse visitors. A map is useful only while we can trust it."

1. What did Leila use to find the mint when its label could not be read?（2分）
2. What two things does Leila check every Friday?（2分）
3. What do you think of Leila's weekly check? Give a reason based on the passage.（3分）
