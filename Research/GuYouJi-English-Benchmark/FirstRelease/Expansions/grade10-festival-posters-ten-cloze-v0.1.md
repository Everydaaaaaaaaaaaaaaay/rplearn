完整参考回填：

On the morning of our community festival, I saw a man taking brightly coloured posters off the noticeboard. I had spent two evenings painting one of them and took his action as an insult, so I felt upset. Instead of asking for an explanation, I told him to leave the board alone. He pointed to a cardboard box beside him, but I could barely control my temper. I was too angry to look inside.

I walked away, still believing that the drawings would never appear at the festival. All our effort seemed to have been wasted. Then heavy rain began. I returned to the noticeboard, expecting to find damaged paper. The board was empty, but the box was now under a roof. Inside it, our posters were neatly rolled up and completely dry.

The man had put them away before the rain. He was protecting our work, not throwing it away. My face grew hot as I thought about my accusation. Embarrassed, I apologised, and he accepted. The posters were for an auction supporting the community centre, so we both wanted them to be seen. When the rain stopped, we put them back together. That afternoon taught me to ask before I judge, rather than treat my first impression as the whole story.
