## 作者核对

### 第1段完整原创脚本

R: When Lena Ortiz began making sound effects for small films, she thought the task was to find an object that produced exactly the sound shown on the screen. Years later, she describes that assumption as her first useful mistake. A real door being shut may sound disappointingly weak in a recording, while an unrelated cupboard can suggest the weight of a much larger entrance. Lena's job is not simply to reproduce an object in isolation, but to help the audience understand what is happening in a scene. For one film, a character had to cross an empty room without waking somebody. Lena initially recorded a sequence of clear footsteps. The director rejected it, not because the sounds were technically poor, but because the character seemed careless rather than cautious. In the version that was used, small movements of clothing and a chair were more noticeable than the feet. Lena now runs occasional workshops in which participants create different soundtracks for the same silent scene. She does not ask them to identify the single correct household object for each noise. Instead, they compare how their choices alter the character's apparent intention. Students sometimes find that an impressive effect makes a scene less convincing because it draws attention away from the action. Lena says technical skill remains essential, but the point of acquiring it is to make a choice serve the film, rather than to make every sound advertise the skill of its maker.

### 第1题：A

录音问题：What did Lena come to reconsider about making sound effects?

A：早先exact sound assumption是有益错误；B是如今核心目标未放弃；C技能仍必需；D仍用家中物，不是否定。

### 第2题：B

录音问题：Why did the director reject Lena's first soundtrack for the room scene?

B：clear footsteps令角色像careless而非cautious；A恰有脚步；C拒绝原因非物声种类缺项；D明确非技术差。

### 第3题：C

### 第2段完整原创脚本

R: A small cooperative grocery shop used to regard an unavailable item as a lost sale. If the usual brand of a product had sold out, staff simply apologized and moved on. Its new manager, Amira, asked a different question: were customers seeking that particular brand, or a product that performed a particular job? She invited staff to write down requests they could not meet and, with the customer's permission, to ask what the item was needed for. The answers showed that substitution was sometimes straightforward, but not always. Someone buying flour for a familiar recipe might accept another brand with the same stated characteristics. A customer buying a gift might care about the original packaging and reject a technically similar alternative. Amira therefore warned against treating every empty shelf as an opportunity to recommend whatever was left. The shop produced brief notes explaining which features staff should ask about before suggesting an alternative. These were prompts for a conversation, not instructions to insist that two products were identical. After a month, the shop recorded more purchases following an initial unavailable request. Amira was pleased, but did not claim that the notes alone had caused the improvement: more experienced staff had also been working during that period. She decided to keep a record of both accepted and rejected suggestions. Understanding why a customer declined an alternative, she said, could be as valuable as recording a successful sale, especially if it prevented staff from making the same unsuitable recommendation again.

### 第4题：D

录音问题：What did Amira ask staff to find out about unavailable items?

D：问用途/功能还是指定品牌；A/B/C均非所问，未市场广告/跨店/加价调查。
