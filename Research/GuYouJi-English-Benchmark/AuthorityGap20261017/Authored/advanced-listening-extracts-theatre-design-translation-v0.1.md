## 作者核对

### 1：B

女方以开放日体验认为待客有具体改变，男方质疑普通工作日能否维持，故B。A男方明说剧目不是障碍，未批评冒险程度；C男方明确价格从不是反对理由。

### 2：A

### 3：C

### 5：A

### 6：B

### 音频1完整原创脚本

F: I booked for the reopening. The programme looks adventurous: three new plays and hardly a familiar title. I thought that would finally persuade you to come.

M: The plays aren't what puts me off. Before the closure, the place made newcomers feel they were interrupting a private club. A fresh programme won't necessarily change the welcome at the door.

F: I went to their open afternoon. Nobody assumed I knew where anything was, and the staff actually asked what would make a first visit easier. That seemed a fairly concrete change.

M: Encouraging, but an open afternoon is designed to impress. I'd rather hear from someone who turns up on an ordinary wet Tuesday, when the staff have three things to do at once.

F: Fair enough. I wouldn't call one afternoon proof of a permanent transformation either. But I think it's worth giving them a chance. Besides, the cheaper seats are still available.

M: Price was never my objection. Perhaps I could start with the short production you mentioned. If the evening feels different, I might try one of the longer plays later.

### 音频2完整原创脚本

M: How was the studio placement? You were worried they'd have you making coffee all month.

F: I did make plenty, but that wasn't the problem. They asked me to prepare three versions of a poster, then selected one without telling me why. I could see which version they'd chosen; I couldn't work out what I'd learnt from the choice.

M: Perhaps they expected you to ask. Designers can't explain every decision when a deadline is approaching.

F: I did ask, and the answer was simply that it felt right. I'm not claiming there should have been a lecture after every sketch. A sentence about the intended audience would have given me something to try next time.

M: So it wasn't the amount of responsibility. It was the lack of anything you could carry into the next task.

F: Exactly. They were generous about letting me present ideas. I just needed feedback that went beyond approval or rejection.

M: For your next placement, why not ask how they discuss revisions before you accept? A famous studio name doesn't tell you how someone will help a beginner improve.

F: That's the question I'll be taking to the interview, rather than asking how much of my work gets published.

### 音频3完整原创脚本

F: The new drafting tool has made our first pass much quicker. I'm less certain about the time it saves over an entire job. Some suggestions are so fluent that you have to stop yourself accepting them before you've checked who is promising what.

M: I've noticed that. A rough sentence practically asks to be inspected. A polished one can persuade you the thinking has already been done.

F: Which is why I keep the source beside the draft, even for passages that seem routine. Yesterday an elegant phrase turned a possibility into an undertaking. Nothing sounded awkward, but the relationship between the parties had changed.

M: Would you stop trainees using the tool, then?

F: No. I'd ask them to explain one accepted suggestion and one rejected suggestion. If they can only say that one sounds better, we haven't yet taught the judgement the job requires.

M: That would also stop us treating every correction as a failure of the software. Choosing when not to use a suggestion is part of the translator's work.

F: Exactly. I want the tool to make room for that work, not to give a convincing appearance that it has disappeared.
