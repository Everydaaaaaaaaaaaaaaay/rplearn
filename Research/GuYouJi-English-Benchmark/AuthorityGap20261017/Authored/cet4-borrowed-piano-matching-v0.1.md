# 四级：借来的钢琴留下了什么

## 学生题面

阅读完整A—K十一段长文，为全部十条陈述选择最符合信息所在段落的字母。同一段可以被多条陈述选中，也可以没有陈述对应；不要按用过即排除。情境与人物均虚构。

### A

The piano arrived in the fictional village of Merewick on a wet Tuesday, six months before it was due to leave again. A former resident, Leon, had offered to lend it while his house was being rebuilt. Several villagers remembered the announcement as a gift. The librarian, who had arranged the loan, kept repeating the return date whenever someone thanked Leon for his generosity. This was not ingratitude. She wanted the village to enjoy the instrument without building plans on the mistaken belief that it would belong to them permanently.

### B

The written agreement settled one question while leaving several others open. Leon remained the owner, and the village could not sell the instrument to pay for other activities. It could use the piano for lessons and public performances without asking him about each event. Moving it to another building, however, required a separate conversation. The librarian read these terms at a meeting and put a copy beside the booking diary. Permission to use a borrowed object, she explained, did not necessarily include permission to decide its future. The distinction became important when a cafe later proposed buying it.

### C

Getting the piano into the library required more planning than finding volunteers with strong arms. The transport company asked for measurements of the doors and the narrow turn inside the entrance. A volunteer's first drawing showed only the outside steps, so she returned to measure the corridor as well. The company then chose a different entrance. On delivery day, the team followed that route, while the volunteers kept the public area clear. In the village newsletter, the librarian credited the person who had measured the corridor alongside the driver. Her contribution had been easy to miss because it happened before the visible arrival.

### D

During the first practice, the sound seemed much louder than listeners expected. Some villagers suggested that the piano must have been damaged in transit. The visiting tuner found no evidence of that. She asked the player to repeat the same passage while listeners stood in different parts of the room. Their descriptions changed with their position. It simply stopped them from deciding that an unfamiliar listening experience proved a broken instrument. They moved the audience chairs before the next event, leaving the piano where it was.

### E

The first proposed timetable reserved most evenings for confident performers. Then a parent asked where a child who could play only a few notes would fit. The librarian added practice periods during which no audience would be admitted. Beginners could repeat a difficult line without feeling that they were holding up a concert. These sessions did not promise that everyone would become a performer, and they were not advertised as shows. They gave residents permission to use the instrument while still learning. Some experienced musicians booked them too, explaining that private rehearsal and public performance served different purposes.

### F

Access to the room was a separate matter from permission to book the piano. The library opened late on Thursdays, but an evening booking on another day did not mean that a member of staff would remain at work. After two players arrived to find a locked door, the booking diary gained a second column naming the person responsible for opening and closing. A reservation was confirmed only when both columns were filled. The caretaker did not become responsible for organising musical events; the new record made the practical responsibility for the door visible instead of leaving it to assumption.

### G

One visitor never played a note and rarely stayed for a complete piece. He came after work to sit near the room while his daughter practised. At first he apologised for taking a chair that a musician might need. The librarian told him that being a listener did not require specialist knowledge. His daughter later said that his presence mattered more to her than comments about technique would have done.

### H

A concert by Leon's former teacher drew the largest crowd of the season. The pianist welcomed the audience but declined a request to broadcast the performance on the village website. She had agreed to play in the room, not to have that particular performance distributed online. The organiser accepted her decision and removed a recording device before the concert began. Afterwards, he published a written account with her approval. A full room and an enthusiastic response did not make the earlier agreement broader than it had been. The village could describe the event without supplying a recording of it.

### I

Near the end of the loan, a small dark mark was noticed beside the lid. An argument began over which recent player must have caused it. Before anyone was approached, the librarian compared the instrument with the delivery photographs. The same mark was visible there. She added a dated note to the condition record, and the discussion ended without an accusation. The photographs had not been taken to identify a guilty person. They had recorded what arrived. Their value in this instance lay in preventing a later difference of memory from being treated as evidence of new damage.

### J

Tom had hoped to perform a solo before the piano left. When the programme was announced, his name appeared only as a page-turner for another player. He was disappointed, then discovered during rehearsal that following the music closely enough to turn at the right moment demanded real attention. His short account in the newsletter described that change of view. It did not claim that helping another musician was identical to giving a solo. It explained why a role he had considered secondary became an achievement he could discuss with pride, even though his original ambition remained unfulfilled.

### K

When the transport company returned, nobody suggested hiding the booking diary to delay departure. The piano went back to Leon on the agreed date. The library retained the diary, the condition photographs and a folder of residents' accounts. A small singing group continued to meet without the instrument, while several players arranged lessons elsewhere. The librarian resisted calling these activities a replacement for the piano. They were things people had chosen to continue after sharing it. The loan had ended, but its ending did not require every relationship formed during those six months to end at the same time.

## 作者核对

### 1：B

B明确Leon保留所有权、村里不可出售，并提咖啡馆求购。A仅说借期误认；K归还不讨论出售；其余段分别为运输、音响、排练、开门、旁听、录制、痕迹与翻页，无购买权限。

### 2：D

D先出现响度意外，再调音师核无损、换听者位置，阻止把陌生听感当故障。I也查损坏但对象为旧痕迹不是声音；C运输无听感调查；其余段不含该声学疑问。

### 3：H

H区分同意现场演奏与拒绝网站播送。B是出借物使用/出售权限，未涉及演奏者录制；G旁听、J翻页也未有传播拒绝；其余段无录制许可。

### 4：F

F预约日记增第二栏开关门责任人，两栏填齐才确认。E分练习/演出但不设门禁责任；B协议允许活动不等有人开门；其余段没有该预约条件。

### 5：E

E beginner practice不接纳audience并可反复练难句。F是开门条件非旁观者限制；G是父亲旁听并非新私练时段；其他段不介绍这一时段设置。

### 7：H

### 8：J
