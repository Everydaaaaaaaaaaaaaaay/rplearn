## 作者核对

### 第1段完整原创脚本

R: For nearly thirty years, retired shopkeeper Nora Bell wrote down the weather outside her kitchen window every morning. Her notebooks contain no complicated measurements. She noted whether the road was wet, which hills she could see, and when the apple tree began to flower. After she moved to a smaller home, her grandson suggested throwing the notebooks away. Nora almost agreed, since the information was never intended for anyone else. A local history group persuaded her to keep them. Its members were studying changes in everyday life in the village and found the notes useful alongside photographs and newspaper reports. They did not treat a wet road as a precise measure of rainfall. Instead, the notes sometimes helped explain why an outdoor event was moved or why a particular journey took longer than expected. The group has copied the notebooks and returned the originals to Nora. Some dates are difficult to read, and a few pages are missing, so the collection cannot provide a complete daily record. Even so, it offers details that more formal records often leave out. Nora now writes a sentence about what she did as well as what she saw. She says the group's interest has reminded her that an ordinary day can become interesting when someone looks back at it years later.

### 第2段完整原创脚本

R: When a student group was asked to produce a short documentary, its members quickly divided the work. One would arrange interviews, another would film, and a third would edit the material. Each person estimated how long their own part would take. Added together, the estimates seemed to leave plenty of time before the deadline. The difficulty was that the tasks could not all happen independently. The editor could not begin with the main interviews until the filming was finished, and the filming depended on people agreeing to be interviewed. One cancellation therefore affected much more than a single afternoon. After their first delay, the students drew a simple chart showing which activities had to come before others. They also marked smaller jobs, such as choosing background music, that could be done while they waited for an interview. The chart did not make interviewees easier to contact, and it could not prevent equipment problems. It did, however, help the students see where a delay would matter most. They finished the film a day before the deadline, after removing a planned extra interview. In their final report, the group said its original mistake was treating a list of individual working hours as if it were a timetable. For its next project, it would discuss the order of tasks before promising a completion date.

### 第3段完整原创脚本

R: The Thursday chess club at a community center was originally so quiet that visitors were sometimes unsure whether they were allowed to enter. Experienced players would begin a game immediately, while newcomers watched from a distance. The coordinator, Ben, noticed that several first-time visitors left without asking to play. He decided to introduce a short welcome session before the games. At first, he tried explaining all the rules to the whole room. Regular members became impatient, and beginners found there was too much to remember. Ben then changed the arrangement. Experienced volunteers now sit at a separate table for the first fifteen minutes, where newcomers can try a few moves and ask questions. Regular games continue elsewhere in the room. There is no requirement to attend the welcome table, and visitors who already know the game can join a match straight away. The club also lends small chess sets for use at home, although borrowers must return them before taking another. Ben does not measure the change simply by counting how many people walk through the door. He is more interested in how many first-time visitors return. He says the purpose of the new arrangement is not to turn every visitor into a competition player. It is to make the club a place where people feel comfortable admitting that they are still learning.
