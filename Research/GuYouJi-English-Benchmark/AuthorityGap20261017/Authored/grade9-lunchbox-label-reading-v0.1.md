# 初三：午餐盒回收的新办法

## 学生题面

阅读完整短文，按各题所问范围选择一个最佳答案，保留段落划分及画线词。

In a fictional school, pupils often left lunch boxes on a shelf after eating. Helpers wanted to return the boxes quickly, but several looked exactly alike. A name written on the lid did not always solve the problem, because lids and boxes sometimes became separated.

The helpers tried giving each box and its lid the same small number. They put a list of numbers and owners inside a cupboard. To find the owner's name, a helper read the number and checked <u>it</u> against the list. Everyone's name did not need to appear on a public board. Pupils could still recognise their own numbers.

At first, the numbers were printed on ordinary paper. The writing became difficult to read after washing. Putting larger numbers on the same kind of paper did not solve that problem. The helpers then used labels intended to stay readable after washing. They tested a few for a week before changing all the boxes.

The new labels made matching easier, but they did not tell the helpers where a missing box had been left. So the school kept one collection point near the dining room and asked pupils to bring found boxes there. A clear system needed both a way to identify an item and an agreed place to look for it. The helpers continued to check the system with pupils instead of assuming that every difficulty had disappeared.
