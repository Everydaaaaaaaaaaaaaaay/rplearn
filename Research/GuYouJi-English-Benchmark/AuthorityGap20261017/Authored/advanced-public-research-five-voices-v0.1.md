### What makes a research report trustworthy?

Five contributors discuss the publication of a fictional public-service study. Match each statement to the contributor whose advice it reports.

A — Leila, commissioning editor

When a report is commissioned, the question in its brief matters more than the number of charts it eventually contains. I ask researchers to list the decisions that their evidence could reasonably change before they begin collecting it. That list is provisional, but it makes a later surprise visible. A change of direction is not misconduct; quietly rewriting the original question so that the final result looks inevitable is. I am less worried about a report that ends with a smaller claim than expected than one that has lost any trace of its starting point. The original brief should travel with the finished publication, even when the two no longer fit neatly together.

B — Owen, public information designer

People often tell us that uncertainty should be shown on the first page. I agree that it must be findable, but a wall of qualifications can prevent a reader from discovering the decision that needs attention. We begin with a short account of what is at stake and provide a clearly signposted route to the assumptions and limitations. Nobody has to accept the summary as the whole argument. Our accessibility trial also includes readers who do not work in research. When only specialist colleagues can locate the caveats, the fact that every caveat technically appears somewhere in the document is a poor defence.

C — Priya, evaluation researcher

A persuasive personal account can identify a mechanism worth investigating. It cannot tell me how often that mechanism operates across a service. For that I need a way of selecting cases that does not depend on who has the most striking story. I do not discard testimony: in one recent project, it led us to change the categories in our survey. But I resist the demand to convert every memorable quotation into a claim about typical experience. Before calling an outcome a success, I also want to know what would probably have happened without the programme. A favourable change after an intervention is a beginning for inquiry, not its conclusion.

D — Tomas, community research organiser

If residents appear only after the questionnaire is finished, their contribution is restricted to answering someone else's questions. We invite them while the categories are still open to challenge. This does not mean every suggestion must survive unchanged. We keep a record of proposals we reject and explain why, so that consultation is not confused with a promise of unanimous approval. Payment for that work is part of the budget from the outset. Offering only public thanks tends to favour people who can afford to give time away, which can make an apparently welcoming process remarkably selective.

E — Hana, data steward

Our greatest pressure arrives when an old collection acquires a promising new use. Removing names does not by itself settle whether that use is appropriate; people may have agreed to one purpose and not another. We therefore return to the terms under which the information was supplied before celebrating what a new analysis could reveal. Sometimes access must remain restricted. That is compatible with transparency: we can publish the procedure and the reasons for a decision without publishing the underlying records. A well-intentioned researcher should not receive an exception merely because explaining the limits would delay a project.

## 作者核对

### 1：A

A明确original brief伴随最终出版物，防悄改问题。B讲呈现顺序，C讲采样/反事实，D记录居民建议而非研究原brief，E讲用途许可，均未提出保留初始研究问题与成品同附。

### 2：B

B以非研究读者可找到caveats为可及性测试；A看brief变化、C审研究设计、D谈参与付酬、E谈许可，无该读者导航测试。

### 3：C

C区分个人叙述可提示机制与无法证明频率，另需选择案例方法；A研究问题、B摘要阅读、D协商、E信息用途都未作此机制/普遍性区别。

### 4：D

D明确问卷类别仍可挑战时邀请居民。A让研究者先列决策非居民设计；B非研究者测试找caveats发生文档呈现；C testimony改survey categories看似近，但未主张在类别固定前邀请受影响人群，D直接明说这一参与时点；E用途许可非类别设计。

### 5：E

E明确Removing names does not by itself settle新用途恰当性。A—D均未讨论匿名化与授权。

### 6：A

A反对悄改原问题让结果看似必然，接受改变却要留痕。B强调可找限制非方向变更；C评成效；D拒绝建议记理由并非掩盖研究改向；E受限用途。

### 7：D

D解释payment outset与give time away带来的选择。A委托编辑非付酬，B读者测试无付酬，C选择案例偏差非无偿参与，E审批延迟无预算。

### 8：C

C明确without programme反事实；A可能改变的决策非未干预比较，B信息分层，D设计协商，E数据用途均不问反事实效果。

### 9：B

B短述what at stake＋signposted route assumptions，非把限制隐藏。A原brief随文，C方法推断，D建议记录，E公开程序有透明性但未主张短入口/阅读路线。

### 10：E

E publish procedure/reasons without underlying records。A原brief公开而非限数据，B找caveats非受限记录，C方法边界非信息保密，D公开拒绝建议缘由非限制底层记录。
