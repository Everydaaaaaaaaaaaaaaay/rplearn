# 高一：排练记录的空白

## 学生题面

阅读完整短文，按各题所问范围选择一个最佳答案，保留段落划分。

The following account describes a fictional drama workshop. Its director, Mara, noticed that actors could repeat her instructions accurately and still seem uncertain about the next rehearsal. They remembered comments such as “leave more space” but no longer recalled which moment of the scene each comment concerned. Her notes had preserved the words while losing their connection to an action.

Mara asked an assistant, Felix, to divide a recording into short scenes. Actors could attach a written note to a particular moment and explain what they hoped to try next. Felix's job was to make those moments easy to locate; he was not asked to decide whether an interpretation was dramatically convincing. That judgement remained with the people rehearsing together.

The workshop described the new record as “a set of handles, not a finished performance”. The handles helped actors pick up an interrupted line of work. A marked moment could remind them where an idea had arisen, but it could not make the artistic choice for them. Two actors might attach different notes to the same pause, and that difference could start a useful conversation rather than indicate an error in the system.

During a trial, fewer rehearsal minutes were spent searching for the scene under discussion. The director did not conclude that every performance had therefore improved. Some actors wrote too much and found their own notes hard to use. Mara consequently kept the system optional and introduced a short review at the end of each session, when actors could remove notes that no longer helped. The record was valuable when it supported their attention, not when maintaining it became a second performance demanding attention of its own.
