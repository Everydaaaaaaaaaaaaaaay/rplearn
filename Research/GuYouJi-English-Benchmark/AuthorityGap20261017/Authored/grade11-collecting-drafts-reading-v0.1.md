# 高二：收藏建议与开始写作

## 学生题面

阅读完整短文，按各题所问范围选择一个最佳答案，保留段落划分。

The writer of this fictional column keeps a folder called “For my next story”. It contains advice about openings, interviews with novelists and photographs of possible settings. For a while, adding to the folder feels like progress. Yet the first page of the story remains blank. The writer is preparing to make a choice while carefully postponing the experience of making one.

There is a useful distinction here between gathering material and using the possibility of gathering more as a shelter. A folder is not a problem merely because it is large. It becomes a shelter when each new item supplies a reason to delay an attempt that could already be made. The relevant question is what the collection is enabling, not whether collecting is in itself a respectable activity.

Why might the shelter be attractive? According to the columnist's account, an unwritten story can still contain every appealing possibility. A first draft will be narrower and visibly imperfect. Saving another piece of advice preserves the pleasant image of the future story without exposing a particular sentence to criticism. This explanation does not apply to every delayed project; missing essential information can be a perfectly good reason to wait.

Simply deleting the folder would not necessarily address the hesitation. Nor does the columnist propose a rule that every piece of advice must immediately produce a finished scene. The task is to create a small encounter with the work while leaving room for later learning. In the next section, the writer will offer a short exercise that lets a reader turn one collected item into an imperfect paragraph, without first having to decide the whole story.
