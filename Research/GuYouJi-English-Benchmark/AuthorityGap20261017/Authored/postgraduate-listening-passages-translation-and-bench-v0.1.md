## 作者核对

### 第1段完整原创脚本

R: In this fictional account, translator Lena Moss is preparing a children's story for readers in another language. One character repeatedly uses a phrase that sounds ordinary to adults but is misunderstood by the child in the story. A literal translation preserves the adult meaning, yet in the new language it no longer allows the child's mistaken interpretation. The joke then disappears, and a later scene seems to have no cause. Lena considers replacing the phrase with a different expression that can produce a comparable misunderstanding. This is not a decision to improve the author's plot. It is an attempt to preserve a relationship between two parts of the existing story. She writes to the editor explaining what the literal wording loses and what the alternative changes. The editor asks her to check that the replacement works when spoken aloud, because the book is likely to be read to children. A sentence that looks neat on the page may not make the two meanings equally available to a listener. Lena tries several versions before selecting one. In a note for the publisher, she records the reason for the departure from the literal phrase. The note is not presented as something a child must study before enjoying the story. It is part of the working history of the translation. Lena says fidelity sometimes requires a translator to explain why matching individual words would fail to preserve the action those words perform in a particular narrative.

### 第1题：B

录音问题：What is lost in the literal translation of the phrase?

B新语言不能儿童误解→笑点及后场因果失；A/D无，C成人义反保留。

### 第2题：C

录音问题：Why does Lena consider another expression?

C保原叙事关系，A非加剧情/B她咨询/D不删人物。

### 第3题：A

录音问题：Why does the editor ask for a spoken test?

A常朗读，听到双义需测试；B/C/D无且非全无文字。

### 第2段完整原创脚本

R: A fictional design team developed a bench for a university courtyard. In the studio, it looked comfortable and passed the team's stability checks. The first outdoor trial was less encouraging. Students used the far end as a place to leave bags, and the central seats were often empty. One designer proposed adding a notice telling people to sit in the intended places. Another asked whether the bench was communicating its possible uses clearly enough. The team watched without intervening for several afternoons. It noticed that the centre faced directly into a busy walking route, while the far end offered a little distance from passing groups. The designers then tested a different orientation. More people used the central seats, but the trial also revealed a new difficulty: the changed position partly narrowed the route used by a delivery trolley. The team did not treat the rise in seated users as permission to ignore that obstruction. It adjusted the position again and asked the courtyard staff to test the trolley route before fixing the bench permanently. The project did not produce a universal rule about where all benches should face. It showed how a technically sound object could still fit poorly into one setting, and how improving one observed use could complicate another. The team kept the failed positions in its project record so a later redesign would not repeat the same apparent solution without understanding why it had been abandoned.

### 第5题：A

A正对busyroute，远端距离大；B/C/D无预留锁门/仅批准。

### 第6题：B

录音问题：Why was another adjustment needed after more students used the centre?

### 第7题：C

录音问题：Why did the team retain records of unsuccessful positions?
