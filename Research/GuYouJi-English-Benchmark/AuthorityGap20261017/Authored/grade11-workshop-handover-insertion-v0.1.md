# 高二：把工作交接清楚

## 学生题面

A workshop handover is useful only if the next person can continue the work without reconstructing every decision. A long account is not necessarily a helpful one. The following sequence offers a way to prepare a short, usable record.

#### Say what comes next

(1) ____, so begin with the action that the next person is expected to take. For example, write that three sample cards need checking before printing, rather than filling the first page with the history of the project. Background can follow when it explains a decision the reader needs to make.

#### (2) ____

Once the next action is clear, distinguish settled decisions from choices that are still open. A card's final size may already have been approved, while its colour is awaiting a trial print. Mark that difference instead of describing both as “almost finished”.

#### Examine the example again

Unclear status words can turn a request into an assumption. (3) ____. The reader needs to know that size requires no new decision, whereas the colour still needs checking. A short note can make that distinction more clearly than several cheerful sentences about progress.

#### Match approval to responsibility

Different choices may belong to different people. (4) ____, send the colour result to that person before approving the print run. If the workshop itself has final authority, record its own approval. Passing information on is not the same as acquiring the right to approve it.

(5) ____. Leave space for the next person to confirm what was received and record what they did. The handover then becomes a connection between two stages of work, rather than a document whose accuracy nobody checks after it is sent.
