1. Why is the woman reluctant to order the smaller recorder?
   A. Its weight would make fieldwork impractical.
   B. Its performance in relevant conditions is uncertain.
   C. The supplier has refused to provide any specifications.

2. What do the researchers agree to do?
   A. Select the established model on reputation alone.
   B. Postpone fieldwork until all noise can be eliminated.
   C. Compare both devices in the intended setting.

3. What limitation in the earlier test does the woman identify?
   A. It covered a different pattern of communication.
   B. It relied entirely on photographs of the room.
   C. It involved more speakers than the room could hold.

4. What does the woman want to avoid?
   A. Allowing the client to observe a normal meeting.
   B. Assuming a particular solution before examining the activity.
   C. Considering changes to the room’s physical arrangement.

5. How does the woman view the interviewee’s complaint?
   A. It deserves clarification before its merits are decided.
   B. It proves that the article must be withdrawn.
   C. It should be ignored because hesitation was recorded.

6. What do the speakers agree about their response?
   A. They should promise the wording will be changed.

## 作者核对

### 1：B

展示安静、噪声图不说明研究船边特定声响能否区分，B。A反而更易携带；C已给图表，非拒绝一切规格。

### 2：C

借两机同位置同设置在港口测试，C。A被女人否定；B任务场景本有船，并未要消除所有噪声再研究。

### 3：A

此前只测单人presentation，实际为多人抢话讨论，A。B照片是外界评价非测试方法；C实际需求六人但未说人数超容量。

### 4：B

先看日常活动再诊断，不先把麦克风当定案，B。A正要求旁听；C明确可能两边都改，不排除物理布局。

### 5：A

她要先明确争议点再对录音，未先判正误，A。B不认同全撤；C即使有录音仍需听清何意，不是忽略。

### 6：C

解释核查及回复时间，同时立改公交位置，C。A明确不预先承诺改字；B明确不拖延straightforward correction。

### 音频1完整原创脚本

F: The smaller recorder would be easier to carry, but I'm not ready to order it. The supplier's demonstration was in a quiet room. Our recordings will be made beside working boats.

M: They sent a graph showing how it performs with background noise.

F: Yes, though that tells us about the conditions they selected. I want to know whether we can still distinguish the calls we're studying when an engine starts halfway through one.

M: The larger model has a stronger reputation. Shall we choose that and avoid the risk?

F: Reputation doesn't answer the question either. If it filters out the sound we're interested in along with the engine, a cleaner recording could actually be less useful.

M: Then we should borrow both for an afternoon at the harbour, using the same settings and position.

F: Exactly. I'm not trying to buy the most expensive device or the one with the most impressive graph. I want a comparison tied to the recording decision we'll have to make.

M: I'll ask for a loan. Better to find the awkward case now than after we've spent a week collecting material we can't use.

### 音频2完整原创脚本

M: Everyone liked the photographs of the finished room. Now that we're holding meetings in it, people at the back say they can't hear the discussion near the window.

F: We tested a presentation with a single speaker, and that worked. We didn't test six people interrupting one another around a table. That's the use we need to look at now.

M: The manager wants a microphone for every seat. It sounds decisive, though I'm not sure anyone has asked how those meetings are actually run.

F: More equipment might help, but it could also make the room harder to use. Before choosing a remedy, I'd sit through an ordinary meeting and note where people turn away, move their chairs, or try to speak at once.

M: You think we should change the way we work rather than the room?

F: Not necessarily. We may need to change both. I'd just rather not treat a description of the problem as though it were already a diagnosis.

M: I'll arrange a visit during Tuesday's planning meeting. A room can look finished before we've understood how people are going to occupy it.

### 音频3完整原创脚本

F: The readers' comments are more useful than I expected. One person spotted that we'd described the bus stop as being outside the station when it's across the square. We should correct that promptly.

M: Agreed. I'm less convinced by the demand that we remove the whole article because one interviewee disliked how her hesitation came across.

F: Neither am I, but I want to speak to her before we decide. She may be objecting to a detail we can check rather than asking us to make her sound more certain.

M: So you wouldn't offer to rewrite her remarks immediately?

F: No. I'd ask which part she thinks fails to represent what she meant, then compare that with our recording. Listening seriously doesn't require promising the requested change in advance.

M: That's worth saying in our reply. Otherwise asking for clarification could sound like a polite way of refusing to act.

F: Exactly. We should explain what we're checking and when we'll get back to her. It's possible to be open about the process without pretending we already know the outcome.

M: I'll draft that response, and fix the location error now. We needn't delay the straightforward correction while we consider the more complicated complaint.
