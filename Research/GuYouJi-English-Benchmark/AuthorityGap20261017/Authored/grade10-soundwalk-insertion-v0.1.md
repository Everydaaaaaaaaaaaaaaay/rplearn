# 高一：录制校园声音路线

## 学生题面

A campus sound walk can help a group notice places it usually crosses without much attention. Participants record short sounds and link them to stops on a route. The project needs clear choices about recording, listening and publication.

#### Decide what a stop should reveal

A recording is more useful when it has a purpose. At a busy doorway, for example, the group might want listeners to notice the rhythm of arrivals rather than identify particular speakers. (1) ____ A clear aim can keep the group from collecting long recordings simply because the device has enough space.

#### Keep a location record

A file name such as “new sound 7” gives little help a week later. (2) ____ The notes need not describe every noise, but they should allow another member to find the intended place. Otherwise, a sound may be placed beside the wrong stop on the route.

#### Check what may be shared

A sound made in a public part of a school is not automatically something the group should publish. Follow the school's recording rules and obtain any required permission. (3) ____ Leaving a recording unpublished is a reasonable choice when those conditions have not been met.

Before the final route is announced, ask someone outside the group to follow it. (4) ____ Where a listener cannot locate a stop or understand an instruction, change the route notes. This is a test of the instructions, not a competition to identify the most sounds.

#### (5) ____

Keep a copy of the approved files together with their location notes and permission record. If the route changes, review those links before reusing the recordings. A useful archive preserves the conditions under which its material can be understood and shared.
