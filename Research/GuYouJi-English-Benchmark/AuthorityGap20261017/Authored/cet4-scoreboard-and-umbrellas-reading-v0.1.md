# 四级：记分牌与修伞活动两篇阅读

## 学生题面

阅读两篇完整短文。第1—5题依据Passage One，第6—10题依据Passage Two，每题从A—D中选一个最佳答案，完成全部十题。人物、机构及事件均为虚构。

### Passage One

During a match at a fictional neighbourhood football club, the electronic scoreboard suddenly showed the home team ahead by two goals. Players on the field knew that the teams had scored once each. Some spectators assumed that a goal had occurred while they were looking away, and the announcer nearly read out the displayed result.

The club's new volunteer, Dan, was operating the board. Before accusing him of entering a false score, the organiser asked him to describe what he had done. Dan had pressed the button labelled “restore”, expecting it to recover the current result after a brief power interruption. Instead, it had loaded the result saved at the end of a different match. The equipment was displaying a genuine record, but one belonging to another occasion.

The organiser checked the referee's written score and announced the correction. She also explained why the board had been wrong. Dan offered to stop using it altogether. She disagreed: the error showed that the controls needed clearer instructions, not that every use of the equipment was unreliable. For the rest of that match, a second volunteer compared each change on the board with the referee's record.

Afterwards, the club changed the label beside the button to explain exactly what it restored. It also placed the date of the saved match beside the result when that function was used. These changes addressed the confusion between an old result and the current one. They did not turn the scoreboard into the official judge of whether a goal had been scored; that responsibility stayed with the referee.

At the next training session, Dan demonstrated the misleading display and the correction to new volunteers. He did not describe his original mistake as harmless: it had nearly become a public announcement. Nor did he treat it as evidence that he should never help again. By showing the sequence of actions, he made it possible for others to understand the problem without assuming that someone had deliberately invented a score.

### Passage Two

The umbrella-repair table at a fictional town fair attracted an unexpected queue. Two volunteers had offered to examine broken umbrellas for an afternoon, mainly to show visitors how repair work began. The fair's notice said “Bring an umbrella for repair”, however, and several people understood that as a promise that every item would be returned in working order before the fair closed.

Mina, one of the volunteers, first tried to explain the limits separately to each person. That meant repeating the same discussion while the queue grew longer. Her colleague suggested accepting only the simplest jobs and saying nothing about the difficult ones until later. Mina rejected this: people with damaged umbrellas would still be waiting without knowing whether any work could be attempted.

They placed a new notice beside the table. It distinguished an initial examination from an agreed repair and explained that some jobs would require parts they did not have. Visitors could then decide whether to remain. The volunteers also stopped accepting more items than they could examine before closing. A number on a ticket indicated a place in the examination queue, not a guarantee of a completed repair.

One visitor complained that the clearer notice made the activity sound less helpful. Mina understood his disappointment but thought that an attractive promise was not a substitute for the materials and time needed to fulfil it. An honest description could prevent a person from waiting for a service that the table was unable to provide. It did not make the uncompleted repair itself satisfactory.

By the end of the afternoon, the volunteers had repaired several umbrellas and explained why others could not be fixed there. Their report listed examinations and completed repairs separately. They wanted the fair organisers to see both the interest in the activity and the limits of an afternoon demonstration. Counting every examination as a successful repair would have produced a more impressive total while hiding what visitors had actually received.
