Borrowing a set of equipment as a group can save individual effort, but it creates shared responsibilities. A useful agreement needs to describe the whole journey of the equipment, not just the exciting activity for which it is borrowed.

#### Find out what is included

Read the list supplied by the lender. The photograph on an advertisement may show accessories that are not included in the actual set. (1) ____ Asking before collection is easier than discovering a missing part when everyone is ready to begin.

#### Name the next person

A group may plan to pass the set from one member to another. Do not rely on the phrase “someone will take it”. (2) ____ The record should also show when the change of responsibility happens. That way, a later question has a clear starting point.

#### Allow time for the return

The activity's finishing time and the lender's closing time are different deadlines. (3) ____ Packing, travel and a final check all require time after the equipment is no longer being used. A plan that ignores those steps can fail even when the activity ends punctually.

If one piece is damaged, record what happened and tell the lender rather than passing it silently to the next borrower. (4) ____ It lets everyone discuss an actual problem instead of guessing later who first noticed it.

#### (5) ____

After the return, look at which parts of the agreement worked and which caused confusion. Keep a brief revised version for the next occasion. The aim is a practical routine that the group can improve, not a promise that no problem will ever occur.
