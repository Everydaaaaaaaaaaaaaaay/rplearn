# 高级：会议筹备五位顾问

## 学生题面

阅读同一专题文章中五位虚构顾问的完整意见，为十条陈述分别选择对应顾问A—E。字母可以重复使用；按建议的对象、条件与责任范围判断。

### Before the conference opens

Five fictional consultants discuss a single professional project.

A — Jo, schedule planner

Start by distinguishing what cannot move from what somebody merely prefers not to move. A speaker whose only train arrives at noon presents a different scheduling problem from one who would rather speak after lunch. Put the genuinely fixed commitments into the plan first, then negotiate the preferences around them. The other essential is ownership of changes. I ask for one named person to accept timetable amendments and propagate them to every published version. Letting each session organiser quietly maintain a private correction is a reliable way of producing several conferences that happen to share an address. Flexibility matters, but a change has not been implemented simply because the person who requested it can see it on their own screen.

B — Harun, language adviser

Sending a translator a list of talk titles may look efficient, but a title can be deliberately playful or omit the relationship its audience is expected to know. Give us a short abstract with each one, so that we can establish what the speaker is actually promising. I also ask for potentially disputed terms to be agreed before the final programme is laid out. Altering a central term late can require changes to several descriptions that look unrelated to the organiser. We do not need every sentence frozen months ahead. We need the decisions that affect other decisions made early enough to be used consistently. A common vocabulary should assist the speakers' different arguments, rather than make those arguments sound identical.

C — Eva, access consultant

Inspecting an accessible meeting room in isolation is not enough. Rehearse the whole journey a participant will make, from registration through the session to lunch and any quieter space provided between events. A usable room can still be part of an unusable day. When inviting requests, ask people what conditions or arrangements they need, not for a medical history that explains why they need them. Someone should be able to request a place away from a loudspeaker without composing a personal account for the organising committee. The practical information belongs with the people who can act on it. Curiosity about a participant's circumstances is not, by itself, a reason to collect more information.

D — Marco, purchasing adviser

A quotation with the lowest headline price may become expensive as soon as the programme changes. Compare what suppliers charge for amendments or cancellations, as well as what they charge for delivering the original order. The comparison should concern a plausible conference, not a perfect one in which nothing ever changes. I also restrict who may authorise extra purchases on the day. Helpful volunteers can otherwise make small verbal commitments to several suppliers, none of whom sees the total. This is not an argument for making every request pass through a committee. Give a small number of named people a clear spending authority and ensure suppliers know who they are. Fast decisions and recorded responsibility can coexist.

E — Sue, evaluation adviser

Decide what a successful conference would enable people to do before reading the complimentary comments. Otherwise, the easiest praise to collect can quietly become the definition of success. A cheerful exit survey tells us something useful, but not whether people made the contacts or acquired the information the event was intended to support. I also keep registrations, arrivals and session attendance distinct in the records. A person who booked and never travelled is not the same case as someone who arrived but chose not to enter a particular session. Combining them into one absence figure makes an apparently straightforward number difficult to interpret. Record the distinctions while they are available; trying to reconstruct them from memory afterwards rarely improves the account.

## 作者核对

### 1：C

C明确rehearse whole journey从注册到午餐/安静空间。A排时间硬约束，B译名语境，D采购，E出席记录，都未要求试走整个参与流程。

### 2：A

A先区分cannot move与merely prefers，固定项先入计划。B早定术语、D比较变更价、E预定成效虽也有先后，却不是固定行程与偏好这一区别；C看可达链。

### 3：D

D对照headline price与amendment/cancellation费用。A谈传播时表变更无报价，B术语晚改无供应商价，C功能需求，E评价指标，都不是采购总成本。

### 4：B

B要求每个title附short abstract，防俏皮标题/省略关系造成误译。A交通约束、C功能安排、D报价、E评论均不解释演讲标题语义。

### 5：E

E先定successful conference让人做到什么再读compliments。A固定行程不是成效指标，B统一术语不是评价标准，C需求/D采购也不对照表扬与成功定义。

### 6：D

D明确restrict who may authorise extra purchases，供应商知道有spending authority者。A一人接收的是timetable amendments非采购支出，B译名/C安排/E记录均无支出授权。

### 7：E

E分registrations/arrivals/session attendance且举两种未出席，不能混一absence。A时间安排不等出席测量，B语境、C路线、D采购均未作这两种缺席区分。

### 8：A

A指定一人accept并propagate amendments to every published version，私人屏幕更新不足。D支出记录不传播日程，B术语一致非时表改动，C功能请求、E缺席分类也非此事。

### 9：B

B早定disputed terms，避免后期central term波及多描述。A公开日程传播没有词汇选择，C安排、D供应商、E评价均不处理跨译文术语依赖。

### 10：C

C问conditions/arrangements而非medical history，举远离音箱请求无需个人叙述。其余四人分别论时表、翻译、采购、评价，均无这一数据范围要求。
