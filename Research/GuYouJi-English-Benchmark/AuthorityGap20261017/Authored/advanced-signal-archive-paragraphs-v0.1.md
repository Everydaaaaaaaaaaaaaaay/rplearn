### An archive that refused to speak for everyone

When the fictional harbour archive asked Mira to design an exhibition about its former signal station, she was handed a beautifully restored room and an apparently simple brief: let visitors hear the past. The surviving equipment, however, was silent. Nobody knew precisely what the room had sounded like during an ordinary working shift. Mira was reluctant to fill that silence with a convincing recording whose confidence exceeded the evidence.

(1) ____

That pairing exposed a difference which a single authoritative label would have concealed. The ledger recorded completed transmissions; the notebook lingered over waiting, mistakes and informal assistance. Neither could stand for every worker's experience. Mira therefore proposed an exhibition about how a working history is assembled, rather than an attempt to reproduce one supposedly typical afternoon.

To find out whether visitors could follow this distinction, she devised an experiment. Two groups would encounter the same documents in different sequences. She wanted to observe what each arrangement encouraged people to infer, without telling them beforehand which inference the designers preferred.

(2) ____

Only after both groups had written their first account did the facilitators reveal the missing provenance. Some participants then revised their interpretation; others defended the sequence they had already constructed. What interested Mira was not how many people had been caught out, but which assumptions survived access to better information. A room that merely surprised visitors would not necessarily teach them anything about evidence.

(3) ____

Neither group of critics was dismissed. Mira kept the provenance exercise but made participation optional, and she replaced the invitation to guess a worker's feelings with a request to identify what the documents could and could not establish. The exhibition was becoming less theatrical, yet the disagreement had given it a clearer purpose.

A further problem emerged when former staff helped draft the captions. They agreed about dates and machinery but not about what counted as an ordinary working day. The designers first tried to negotiate a single wording. That produced a smooth paragraph which none of the contributors thought represented their own position particularly well.

(4) ____

These disputed labels were printed beside the relevant objects, not hidden in a separate account of the exhibition's development. Visitors could see the factual point on which the contributors agreed and the interpretation on which they differed. The labels did not imply that every recollection was equally reliable; they showed why some differences could not be resolved by checking a date.

Most of this material was initially available through a tablet. During a trial, however, several visitors spent their time negotiating the device rather than comparing the accounts. Enlarging the buttons improved one difficulty but did nothing for those who preferred not to use a screen at all.

(5) ____

This separate route led to the same documents and disagreements as the digital version. It was deliberately less convenient to maintain: whenever a caption changed, the physical set had to be replaced too. Mira accepted that cost because access to the argument mattered more than directing everyone through an identical interface.

At the end of the trial, a sponsor asked whether longer visits proved that the new exhibition had made people better at evaluating evidence. The team had measured time in the room, but it had not followed visitors into any later task. A report could easily have blurred those two kinds of observation.

(6) ____

Such restraint made the final report less triumphant than the sponsor had hoped. It also made the exhibition's own argument consistent with its practice. The restored signal station had not acquired a definitive voice. Instead, it offered visitors an opportunity to examine the choices through which a voice is made to sound definitive.

### 待插入的完整段落 A—G（有一段不用）

**A**

Instead of removing the conflict, the team asked each contributor to write a short caption and explain the reason for their wording. A shared line stated the agreed facts. Beneath it, two signed alternatives presented the unresolved difference without pretending that it was simply a factual error.

**B**

One group first saw the official record with its source clearly identified. The other began with selected notebook entries, presented without a named author. Each group then received the remaining set of documents, again without any information that would let it identify who had produced them.

**C**

Mira separated a modest finding from a much larger claim. Visitors had spent time comparing accounts during this particular trial; the project had not established a transferable skill. She proposed a later comparison task as a possible way to investigate that question, rather than reporting an improvement that had not been measured.

**D**

Her first step was to place an official transmission ledger beside a trainee's private notebook from the same month. She asked the curators to treat them as different records of work, not to choose the more entertaining one as a script. Their contrasting omissions would become part of the display.

**E**

The building had once been considered for sale to a restaurant owner. A local campaign persuaded the council to retain it, although the campaigners disagreed about whether restoration should include the furniture. Funding for the roof eventually arrived before a decision about the interior had been reached.

**F**

The experiment attracted objections from two directions. Some former workers felt that withholding the sources turned their history into a puzzle with a trick answer. Several teachers, by contrast, worried that inviting emotional guesses encouraged confident invention. For different reasons, both questioned the educational value of the exercise.

**G**

So the archive produced a complete set of large-print document cards and caption sheets, held in a shallow rack beside the entrance. People could take them through the room without signing into a device. This was an alternative means of following the exhibition, not a shortened summary of its conclusion.

### 1：D

D以ledger/notebook这对同时期材料作为先行对象，后段That pairing及两份材料的差异紧接。A是多人标签争议，非这两份记录；B是访客实验，尚未引入且不能给That pairing两份具体记录；C是结论审慎；E是房屋筹款，与ledger/notebook无关；F是批评者两组，非记录；G是访问媒介。

### 2：B

B给两组不同呈现顺序及未透露出处，紧接后段Only after both...reveal missing provenance。A是写标签的工作人员，不是实验参与两组；C为之后报告；D只有两份材料，不包含两组先写解释及隐藏出处的安排；E讲旧筹款；F有两组批评者但未设置实验/隐藏出处；G是可选纸卡而非两组实验程序。

### 3：F

F引入前员工与教师两组批评，后段Neither group of critics逐组回应：不再强制参与、去掉猜感受。A的contributors争议尚未引入且非这两组教育批评；B是实验两组非critics；C不是批评内容；D是文献配对；E筹款争论不能引出provenance exercise与emotional guessing；G是媒介替代。

### 4：A

### 5：G

G承接平板阻碍，提供完整纸卡/大字页替代，后段This separate route及更新physical set有先行对象。A仅给标签内容分歧，不能解决屏幕操作；B实验顺序不是第二访问媒介；C成效审慎；D增加原件记录不是纸卡路线；E历史房屋计划非访问替代；F批评活动，不引入same documents的实体替代版。
