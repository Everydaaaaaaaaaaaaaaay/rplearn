# 高三：维修储备率的分母

## 学生题面

阅读完整短文，按各题所问范围选择一个最佳答案，保留段落划分。

The following article presents a fictional proposal for describing a community workshop. Its managers want to report how much of their income is set aside for replacing worn tools. They call the measure a repair-reserve share: the money allocated to the reserve during a year, divided by the workshop's total income in that same year, multiplied by 100. It measures an allocation, not the number of repairs completed.

In the example, the workshop receives 80,000 units and allocates 12,000 to the reserve. The share is therefore 15 per cent. Its ordinary running expenses are 48,000 units, but those expenses are not the denominator in this calculation. Dividing by them would answer a different question, however plausible the resulting percentage might look on a poster.

Supporters say the measure makes a neglected commitment visible. A crowded workshop may look successful while leaving nothing for the tools on which next year's activity depends. The share can start a discussion about that future capacity. Critics reply that a high percentage could result from a sudden fall in income rather than a deliberate increase in money reserved. Comparing the percentages alone can conceal that difference. Showing both sums alongside the share would make it easier to examine.

This is why the proposal's author describes the measure as a question-starter. It gives a reader a defined relationship to investigate, not a complete judgement of the workshop. A reserve cannot explain whether classes are welcoming or tools are well used. The report would therefore place the share beside descriptions of activities and maintenance needs, without combining them into one overall score. The purpose is to make one financial choice easier to inspect while keeping the limits of that choice visible.
