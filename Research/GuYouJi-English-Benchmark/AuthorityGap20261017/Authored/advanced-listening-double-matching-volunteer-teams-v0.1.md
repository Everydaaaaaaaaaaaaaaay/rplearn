### 音频1完整原创脚本

S1: When I arrived in this town, I knew the person who'd rented me a flat and almost nobody else. The community garden seemed a good place to have a conversation without arranging a formal social evening. I wasn't particularly interested in learning to grow things, and I expected to leave once I'd found my feet. That was a year ago. Now, if someone misses a Saturday, I wonder whether they're all right. I still couldn't give you a confident lecture about vegetables, but I know whose son has started college and who needs a lift home. I thought I was signing up for an activity. I hadn't expected these particular people to become such an important part of my week.

S2: I'd retired from accountancy and was rather tired of being told how wonderful it must be to do absolutely nothing. I didn't want a new career or another qualification. I wanted the skills I'd spent forty years developing to be useful somewhere. A food charity needed someone to sort out its records, so I offered. The pleasant discovery has been how much room they've given me to change things. I suggested testing a simpler collection form, and they said, try it for a month. In my old office, that would have required several layers of approval. Here I can make a sensible suggestion and find out whether it works. That freedom makes the unpaid role surprisingly absorbing.

S3: My father spent a long time in hospital, and the difficult part for him was being cut off from ordinary conversation. After he came home, I joined a team that organises visits for people in a similar situation. It wasn't about gaining experience for a job; I already had one I liked. I knew what those empty afternoons could feel like from sitting beside his bed. The visits themselves are rewarding. The frustrating part is how long it takes to match a new volunteer with someone waiting for company. I understand why the checks and introductions matter, but I keep thinking about the person still waiting while another form moves between desks. I wish that part could move faster.

S4: I used to sing in a choir where every rehearsal seemed to end with somebody comparing auditions. I wanted my spare time to stop feeling like another competition. Helping at the library's weekend reading club offered exactly that: no rankings, no prizes, just children enjoying stories. I'd assumed it would be a quiet little commitment that nobody outside the room would notice. Then a parent mentioned us in the town newsletter, and strangers started recognising the club's name when I said where I volunteered. I hadn't expected that kind of attention for something so small. It's rather touching. I'm still not looking for a starring role, but it's nice to discover that the effort matters beyond the people who turn up each Saturday.

S5: When the council announced that the neighbourhood pool might shut, a group of us began helping with reception and local fundraising. I joined because losing it would leave this part of town without a place to swim. Friends suggested I was doing it for my children's sake, but they'd already moved away; I wanted the pool to remain for whoever lived here next. I was worried that volunteering would swallow every free evening. In fact, there's a clear rota, and someone else takes over when my two hours are up. I can support the place and still keep the other things in my life. That balance has been a real relief, and it's probably why I've stayed involved.
