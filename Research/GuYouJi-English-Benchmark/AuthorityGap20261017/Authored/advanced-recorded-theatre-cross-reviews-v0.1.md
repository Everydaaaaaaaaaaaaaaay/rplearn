# 高级：同一本剧场录制书的四种评价

## 学生题面

阅读同一本虚构书的四篇完整评论，根据评论者之间的相同或不同观点完成四题。A—D可重复选择；注意other排除题干指定的锚评论者，未表态不等于反对或赞同。

### After the Curtain

Four fictional reviewers discuss the same fictional book.

A — Clara Moss

The book usefully explains how a recording can support the memory of a performance, but it cannot replace being present at the event. Its best proposal is to place the original programme information alongside the video; that written context is an essential part of a responsible archive, not an optional decoration. I am also glad that the author resists cleaning away the audience's audible responses. A laugh or a prolonged silence can illuminate the exchange between stage and auditorium. On the proposal to let viewers select camera angles, however, I remain unconvinced. Making viewers choose shots turns them into editors and interrupts the developing rhythm of the recorded account. A carefully chosen sequence should offer them something to watch rather than a continuing technical decision to make.

B — Simon West

No video is an adequate substitute for attending a play, and the book is right to distinguish an archive from the event it documents. It is right, too, to require the original written programme to accompany the recording. Without that information, later viewers can misunderstand the circumstances in which the production was made. I disagree about retaining sounds from the audience. For an archive intended for close study, those reactions should be removed wherever possible so that speech and music remain clear. Giving viewers control over camera angles is a much more productive form of involvement. It permits repeated study of different parts of a scene. That new possibility makes recording valuable in its own terms, without making it the same experience as a live performance.

C — Leon Park

The most promising idea here is the option to choose among camera angles. It gives viewers an active way to revisit a performance and to follow an action that the main edited version may overlook. Audience reactions should remain audible for a related reason: they are part of what happened, and removing them would make a social event sound like a private rehearsal. My reservation concerns the requirement to include the original written programme. It may be interesting, but it is not essential to a useful recording; the work on stage can sustain an account of its own. Even the most flexible recording, however, remains a distinct encounter. I cannot accept that it could fully substitute for the experience of sharing a theatre with the performers and the original audience.

D — Vera Kent

The author is more cautious than the evidence warrants. A sufficiently accomplished recording can, in my view, provide a fully adequate substitute for attending the play. Physical presence is not a condition of appreciating everything that makes the production significant. This is precisely why I oppose the invitation to switch freely between camera angles. A viewer should be carried through a considered interpretation, not distracted by the need to construct one shot by shot. The audible audience belongs in that interpretation. Its reactions should be preserved, since a laugh can reveal a relationship between performers and spectators that a solitary reading would miss. The book's account of microphone placement is illuminating, although its practical discussion is more detailed than some readers will need.

## 作者核对

### 1：D

D明确fully adequate substitute且不要求physical presence，A/B/C均否认完整替代；D赞成保留观众音不等于要求观看者也必须现场。

### 2：A

A与B均明确essential/require原节目文字随录制；C说可有趣但not essential，D没有对此项表态。other排除B，未提D不当反对，也不以未知补赞同。

### 3：B

C要保留社会事件声音，A与D也主张保留；只有B为近距研究要求尽量移除。B对现场不可替代的观点不改变此项去留判断。

### 4：A

D反对自由选镜头要求跟随成熟编辑解读；A也说选择使观众变编辑并打断节奏。B/C欢迎选角度以重看场景；other排除D，唯一A。
