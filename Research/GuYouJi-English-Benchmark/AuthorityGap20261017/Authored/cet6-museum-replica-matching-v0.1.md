# 六级：博物馆复制品的身份

## 学生题面

阅读完整A—O十五段论述，为全部十条陈述选择最符合信息所在段落的字母。同一段可以被多条陈述选中，也可以没有陈述对应；不要按用过即排除。文章、人物、机构及所述作品均虚构，所有必要信息在题内。

### A

The fictional museum of Dunmere commissioned a replica of a small ceramic cup because its education team wanted visitors to examine something they could actually hold. The original remained part of the collection. Its replacement in certain activities was not intended to conceal an absence, although the word “replacement” soon created confusion. Some visitors understood it to mean that the museum had disposed of the earlier object.

### B

Even the original cup's description was less settled than its neat display label suggested. An early catalogue assigned it to a particular century on the basis of a resemblance to other vessels. There was no dated account of its manufacture. The curator retained that estimate but removed a sentence presenting the date as certain. The revised account distinguished an object's physical presence from the confidence of claims made about its past.

### C

A letter accompanying the original donation introduced a further complication. It called the cup a farmer's drinking vessel, whereas a family diary associated it with a travelling merchant. Neither document explained how its writer had obtained that information. The curator displayed summaries of both accounts and identified their sources. She did not solve the disagreement by choosing the earlier document automatically. Age alone could not establish that its writer had known more.

### D

The ceramic artist who made the replica placed her own initials beneath its handle, where they could be found without opening a case. She refused to imitate a crack that might weaken an object intended for handling. Her workshop record also retained photographs of failed attempts before the final cup was accepted. These decisions made the work a documented act of interpretation rather than an attempt to pass off a newly made vessel as an old one.

### E

Weight presented a different choice. A hollow replica matched the outline well but felt lighter than the original when the team compared them. For a handling session about balance and grip, that difference mattered more than the exact shade of the surface. The artist therefore produced a second version with a similar measured weight, while labelling the material difference. The museum did not claim that equal weight made the substances identical.

### F

At a staff demonstration, two participants tried to identify which of two objects had come from the collection. Both chose incorrectly. A manager suggested using the result to advertise the replica as indistinguishable from the original. The education officer declined. Two responses in an informal exercise, without any agreed procedure, could not establish what all visitors would notice. More importantly, accurate identification was not the handling session's aim. The officer recorded the demonstration as a discussion prompt, not as a measured success rate for the replica or evidence that people could always be deceived.

### G

During a school visit, the teacher initially asked pupils to imagine that the handled cup had travelled through centuries of use. The guide interrupted politely and identified it as the modern replica. Pupils could discuss the shape and the feel of the object in their hands, but its surface was not physical evidence of ancient wear. The correction did not end the activity. It changed the question from “What has this object experienced?” to “What can this copy help us investigate?”

### H

A label beneath the replica's base identified it clearly to someone holding it. That label disappeared when a photograph was cropped for a publicity banner. The museum withdrew the image and issued an official photograph with “modern handling replica” in its caption, requiring that caption to accompany any reuse of that photograph. The base label remained useful in the room, while the caption addressed a different setting. One solution did not replace the other. Information attached to a physical object could not be assumed to survive every way in which an image of it might circulate.

### I

After many sessions, the replica's painted surface became worn near the handle. Staff noted the change before deciding whether to repaint it. Wear on this new object was evidence of recent handling, not newly discovered evidence about the old cup. Keeping separate records prevented a photograph made for a maintenance discussion from later appearing in a historical account with the wrong explanation. The same visible mark could support a claim only when its object and date were also known.

### J

The invoice prompted questions about what the museum was paying for. Clay and pigment represented only a small share of the total; the artist had also charged for researching the shape, preparing trial versions and documenting decisions. A donor proposed comparing the price with the cost of a mass-produced cup. The curator replied that such a comparison would omit much of the commissioned work. This did not establish that every charge was reasonable. It identified what would have to be considered before either a cheap or an expensive final object could be assessed fairly.

### K

Plans to display the original beside the replica were postponed while its protective case was undergoing maintenance. The education sessions continued with the handled version, but publicity stated that the original was temporarily unavailable to view. Staff resisted describing the copy as making the interruption irrelevant. Visitors who had travelled specifically to see the collection object had a different expectation from pupils attending an activity. Explaining the distinction gave those visitors information with which to make their own plans, rather than deciding on their behalf that one encounter was equivalent to another.

### L

The project's first internal report was organised around the artist's sequence of tasks. Volunteers reading it asked where visitor questions would fit. The next edition added those questions beside the relevant decisions, including why a crack was absent and why the colour differed. This changed the report's arrangement without retrospectively changing what the artist had done. Workshop documentation explained the making process; visitor questions revealed what still required explanation in public.

### M

An enthusiastic newspaper headline called the handling session an opportunity to drink from an ancient cup. The article itself mentioned the replica farther down, but the museum requested a correction to the headline as well. The wording attached the ancient object's identity to a different, modern vessel. A qualification hidden in the body could not be expected to correct every reading of the headline. The newspaper subsequently changed it to describe handling a replica of a collection object.

### N

Two years later, the artist revised the replica after a new measurement clarified the handle's angle. The museum assigned the revision a new version number and retained the earlier model with its documentation. It did not erase the first set of measurements or suggest that the original cup had changed. A visitor comparing workshop photographs could now establish which replica each showed. Improving a representation and preserving the record of an earlier representation were compatible aims.

### O

The project left the curator dissatisfied with the question of whether the replica was “real”. It was a real object made by a named artist, a partial representation of a collection object and a tool for particular activities. None of those descriptions made it the original cup. Equally, calling it merely false would hide the work and knowledge that had produced it. The useful inquiry concerned which claims each object could support, in which setting and with what accompanying information.

## 作者核对

### 1：D

D艺人主动刻自身initials并拒伪装古物，明确现代制作身份。H标签也区分复制品但未提供制作人，B年代归属不指现代作者；其他段无制作人身份标记。

### 2：H

H底座标识被裁图遗漏，随后另配官方图注。D初字位置非图裁切，M是新闻标题身份混用，I维护记录不谈图注；其余段无裁图丢标。

### 3：B

B以相似性估计世纪、无制造日期记录，实体在馆不把历史归属变事实。C分歧是使用者职业，N是复制品版本测量；其他段不涉及原物年代确定性。

### 4：K

K专程看原藏品者与活动学生期待不同，原件展柜维护时如实通知。G学生被引导辨copy功能但无专程观原件者，A只讲项目目的；其余段无这组期待与暂不可见的对照。

### 5：E

E为balance/grip调相近实测重量并标材料不同，不宣称成分相同。D避免弱裂纹非重量用途，F识真伪小演示非材料主张；其他段未述这一选型理由。

### 6：N

N新handle角复制品另版本号、留旧模及文档，不覆写旧测量。L改报告编排但无新实物版本，I维护非本次改模；其余段无该新旧模型并存。

### 7：H

H官方照片图注随复用、底座标签同时保留，处理线上与现场两场景。第2题问裁图失去标签，本题问两措施并存及图注随用，事实不同且原指令许复选H。D/M各有标识问题但没有该并行发布要求；其余段无此安排。

### 8：F

F仅两参与者、无预定程序，不能宣称所有访客分不清；不记为成功率。E物理比较未推广识别率，G学生活动不是识真伪实验；其他段无两人试验及拒外推。

### 9：J

J账单除材料还含研究、试模、记录，量产杯价比较遗漏劳动，但未认定所有收费合理。D也有试模记录却没有价格/账单，其他段无定价评估。

### 10：D

D保留accepted final前失败尝试照片；第1题问作者身份，本题问未选过程保留，非同断言重复。N保留的是后来改版前成品，不是制作中失败尝试；L改报告不讲试模；其余段无该失败制作记录。
