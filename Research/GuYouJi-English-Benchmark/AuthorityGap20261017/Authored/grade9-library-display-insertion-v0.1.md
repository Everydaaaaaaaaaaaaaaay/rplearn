A class exhibition does not have to show only the most polished work. A draft can help visitors understand how an idea changed. Before choosing objects, agree whether the exhibition is meant to celebrate finished work, explain a process, or do a little of both. (1) ____

#### Give drafts a voice

Ask each contributor to write a short note about one revision. A useful reminder is, “(2) ____” The visitor should be able to see why the change mattered, rather than merely being told that the later version is better.

#### Make comparisons easy

Place a first attempt near its later version. (3) ____ If the two are on opposite walls, a visitor has to remember details while walking across the room. Clear labels can connect them, but putting them together removes that unnecessary task.

#### Keep a return record

Some objects belong to pupils who want them returned after the exhibition. Record the owner's name on a private list and give each object a matching number. (4) ____ This is a return record, not a public ranking of contributors.

#### (5) ____

When the exhibition ends, invite contributors to say what they learnt from looking at one another's drafts. Keep suggestions about the arrangement for next time. Afterwards, use the private list to return the objects to their owners.
