## 作者核对

### 第1段完整原创脚本

R: In the fictional village of Mere, a volunteer group replaced a faded walking sign. The old sign pointed towards a footbridge that had been closed for repair. The volunteers first painted a bright new arrow in the same direction. It was easier to see but still led walkers to a locked gate. A resident pointed out that visibility was not the only problem. The group then checked the open route and placed the arrow at the junction where walkers actually needed to turn. They added a short notice explaining the temporary change. Before treating the work as finished, they asked someone unfamiliar with the route to follow it. The visitor reached the bridge by the alternative path, but stopped at one point because the notice used a local place name missing from her map. The volunteers added the name shown on the map as well. They kept the old local name, since residents still used it. The result was not a larger collection of arrows at every tree. It was a smaller set of signs tied to the decisions a walker had to make. The volunteers agreed to check them again when the original bridge reopened, because a useful temporary instruction can itself become misleading when the situation changes.

### 第2段完整原创脚本

R: A fictional college film club used to select its programme by asking members to name favourite films. The list was lively, but the discussions afterwards were often short. Members knew whether they liked a film, yet they had little reason to compare particular scenes. A new organiser asked each person proposing a film to add one question that could be discussed using evidence from it. The question did not have to be complicated. It might concern why a character changes a decision or how a final scene affects an earlier one. The club still voted on which films to show; the questions were not used to rank members by intelligence. After the first screening under the new arrangement, the organiser noticed that several quieter members referred to the prepared question when joining in. She was pleased, but did not claim that one meeting proved everyone had become a more skilled critic. Some questions worked better than others, and one had assumed an event that never occurred in the film. The club corrected that question and kept a note of the change. Preparing a discussion, the organiser concluded, meant giving people a shared starting point while remaining willing to revise it after actually watching together.

### 第3段完整原创脚本

R: At a fictional neighbourhood reading room, the lower shelves were often empty by midday while books on the highest shelf remained untouched. The manager first assumed that readers preferred the subjects placed below. To check, she exchanged the positions of two equally small collections for a week. Borrowing increased for the collection moved down and decreased for the one moved up. This did not establish that subject never mattered, but it gave her a reason to examine access as well as content. She watched how people used the room and noticed that some could not comfortably reach the upper shelf. Others could reach it but could not read the labels from the floor. The room then kept frequently requested books within easier reach and placed a clear list of the remaining titles at eye level. Readers could ask a volunteer to retrieve a title from above. The manager did not simply fill the lower shelf with every book, since that would have made the collection crowded and difficult to search. She also kept records of requests and borrowing, so the arrangement could be changed when readers' needs changed. The first explanation had concerned taste. The revised arrangement recognised that an item cannot be chosen easily if a reader cannot find or reach it. Better access did not decide people's preferences for them; it gave those preferences more opportunity to be expressed.
