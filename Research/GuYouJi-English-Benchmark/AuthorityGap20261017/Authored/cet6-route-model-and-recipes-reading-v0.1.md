# 六级：路线模型与食谱知识两篇阅读

## 学生题面

阅读两篇完整短文。第1—5题依据Passage One，第6—10题依据Passage Two，每题从A—D中选一个最佳答案，完成全部十题。人物、机构、研究、历史案例及事件均为虚构；所需信息全部在题内。

### Passage One

A fictional delivery cooperative is considering a route-planning system that reduces the total time spent on the road in a computer simulation. Its designer describes the result as an improvement in efficiency. A driver representative agrees that the calculation may be correct, but asks which journeys have become shorter and which have become longer. The two speakers are not necessarily disputing the same fact. One is describing the sum of recorded journey times; the other is asking how changes are distributed among the people making them.

In the simulated schedule, a driver who previously finished near home now ends across town. Her final delivery takes less time, but returning from it requires a longer journey. The cooperative's original comparison excludes journeys made after the last delivery, on the assumption that these are outside the paid service. That boundary makes the calculation easier to reproduce. It does not make the excluded time disappear from the driver's day. Whether it should enter the assessment is a question about the service's obligations, not something that the computer can settle merely by adding more accurately.

The designer responds by adding a separate column for journeys home. He keeps the earlier result rather than quietly replacing it, so members can see how the choice of boundary changes the comparison. The revised table does not identify a single best schedule for every purpose. A plan with the lowest total may place a particularly heavy burden on a small number of drivers, while a more even distribution may require slightly more travel overall. Choosing between those plans involves a judgment about priorities that should not be concealed inside the word “efficient”.

Nor do the simulations establish what will happen on a working day. Their travel estimates come from a limited set of recorded journeys and do not include every possible road closure. This is a reason to test the selected plan cautiously, not a reason to pretend that modelling has supplied no useful information. It has made certain consequences visible before anyone is asked to adopt the schedule.

The cooperative therefore postpones a vote until the two time measures are displayed together and members have agreed which obligations matter. Its aim is not to replace calculation with personal preference. It is to ensure that a calculation answers an openly chosen question. An accurate answer to a narrowly defined question can remain valuable while being insufficient for the broader decision to which somebody wants to apply it.

### Passage Two

In a fictional account of a bakery apprenticeship, a teacher gives two learners the same written recipe. They weigh the listed ingredients equally and follow the printed sequence, yet the resulting dough feels different. One learner concludes that measurement is useless. The other concludes that one of them must have ignored the instructions. Their teacher regards both conclusions as premature. A written procedure may specify some variables precisely while leaving others outside its description. Equal compliance with what is printed does not establish equality in everything that could affect the result.

The teacher asks the learners to record the flour batch, room conditions and time between mixing and shaping. She is not inviting them to abandon the recipe whenever an outcome disappoints them. She wants to discover which unrecorded differences might explain this particular contrast. Their first notes reveal that the two mixtures had rested for different periods. That observation suggests a possible explanation, but it does not by itself show that resting time was the only relevant difference. The learners agree to keep other recorded conditions as similar as possible in their next comparison.

Alongside these records, the teacher uses phrases such as “until it feels ready”. Such language irritates a visiting editor, who wants instructions that a beginner can follow without already possessing the teacher's experience. The teacher accepts the criticism. She adds descriptions and photographs of several stages, explaining what she notices when judging readiness. The added material is not a numerical replacement for every practical judgment. It is a way to make some of that judgment discussable and easier for another person to learn.

The editor, in turn, abandons the idea that deleting every reference to experience would necessarily make the account more exact. An instruction can be short and measurable while failing to specify a condition that matters. Conversely, appealing vaguely to expertise can protect a teacher's decisions from examination. The revised recipe keeps measurements and explanations together, identifying which are fixed requirements and which are cues to be interpreted in context.

By the end of the apprenticeship, the learners have neither disproved the value of recipes nor discovered a formula that guarantees identical results under all conditions. They have gained a more demanding conception of following instructions: preserving what is specified, noticing what is not, and making a reasoned inquiry when the two fail to explain an outcome. Precision and completeness have become separate questions, each worth asking.

### 1：D

首段一方看sum、一方问各driver分配，D正确。C不是两组driver总量数据之争；A首段并未争哪些delivery进入样本，返程界线是后续具体例；B双方可同意计算正确，非加法精度定义冲突。

### 2：D

第二段即使最后delivery更短，排除的返程仍构成实际负担，D正确；B未给全天各项总时长，不能证明totalworkingtime增加；C尚需agree obligations非已同意；A作者说是责任判断问题，未直接裁commute必属paid。

### 4：B

第四段局部记录/封路缺项要求cautious testing，B正确。C非放弃可复现记录；D加commute处理边界而不补所有封路情境；A否认模型仍能让部分后果提前可见。

### 5：C

### 8：D

### 9：A

### 10：C
