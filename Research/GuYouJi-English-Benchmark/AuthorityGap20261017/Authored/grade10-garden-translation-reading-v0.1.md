# 高一：双语花园牌的分工

## 学生题面

阅读完整短文，按各题所问范围选择一个最佳答案，保留段落划分。

This fictional account concerns a small public garden preparing signs in two languages. Its volunteers initially fed short plant descriptions into a translation tool and printed the results. The difficulty was not simply that a few words were wrong. Some descriptions used “here” and “this corner” even though the printer had combined sentences from different locations. A fluent sentence could therefore point a visitor towards the wrong bed.

The garden manager, Rosa, changed the process. A volunteer first photographed the intended location and wrote a short explanation of what a visitor should learn there. A translator then used that explanation and the draft text together. The software could suggest wording, but responsibility for checking the reference remained with the person reviewing the sign at its actual site. The printer received only the approved version.

Rosa called the photograph “an anchor for the words”. She did not mean that every object in the image had to be described. The image kept a phrase such as “on your right” attached to a particular viewpoint. Without that link, a sentence could be correct in grammar and still give incorrect guidance. Nor did the photograph supply the translation itself; it helped a reviewer ask whether the translation referred to the right thing.

The new procedure took longer before printing. It also caught several mismatches that a check of spelling alone had missed. Rosa did not treat those examples as proof that the tool was useless. The volunteers still used its suggestions, especially when comparing short alternatives. They had simply stopped treating a smooth first draft as a finished sign. Later feedback from visitors would be needed to find out whether the approved signs were easy to use as well as correctly located.

1. Why did the first signs sometimes mislead visitors?
   A. The printer had combined descriptions from different places.
   B. Every plant name had been removed.
   C. Visitors were required to translate the signs themselves.
   D. The garden had no paths between the beds.

2. Who was responsible for checking a sign’s reference at its site?
   A. The visitor arriving for the first time.
   B. The printer working from any draft.
   C. The software choosing the smoothest sentence.
   D. The person reviewing the sign there.

3. Why is the photograph compared to an “anchor”?
   A. It replaces both language versions.
   B. It ties the wording to a specific viewpoint.
   C. It lists every object that a sign must mention.
   D. It guarantees that visitors will like the sign.

4. What can be concluded about the new procedure?
   A. It eliminated the need for human review.
   B. It proved that translation tools have no value.
   C. It used software suggestions within a more careful checking process.
   D. It established that all visitors found the signs easy to follow.
